Nvidia agrees to pay $290 million to end an eight-year lawsuit over crypto-mining GPU sales

Nvidia has agreed to pay $290 million to settle an eight-year securities class action alleging it misled shareholders about how much cryptocurrency mining drove demand for its gaming GPUs in 2018, Chip Dispatch reported on November 27, 2026, citing Tom’s Hardware. The company did not admit wrongdoing, and a Tuesday court filing says the payment will be made in September. Investors alleged Nvidia counted mining demand as ordinary gaming sales; in November 2017 Jensen Huang described crypto’s contribution as “small but not zero.” After Nvidia cut guidance on Aug. 16, 2018, and acknowledged heavy mining use of GeForce cards, its shares fell 28.5% over two trading sessions. A district court dismissed the case in 2021, but the Ninth Circuit partly reversed that ruling and revived the claims. The settlement ends the suit without a court deciding whether the disclosures were misleading.

Belgium’s Wallonia orders Google to stop work at a nearly finished data center over a cooling-water fight

Inspectors from Belgium’s Wallonia region gave about 300 workers a verbal stop order at Google’s Farciennes data center site on Thursday, Nov. 26, where a 30,000-square-meter building was nearly complete, Chip Dispatch reported on November 27, 2026. Google holds a 2024 permit for three air-cooled data centers on the 53-hectare site, but later sought to merge two buildings into one larger center cooled with water from the River Sambre; Walloon planning minister François Desquesnes refused that change in August, citing water demand. Google has appealed to Belgium’s Council of State, with a hearing set for Jan. 7. Greenpeace Belgium said satellite images showed construction continuing on Nov. 25 and filed an urgent complaint a day before the inspection. Google told RTBF it was “committed to an open and collaborative dialogue” with the regional government. The order follows a Nov. 24 Virginia ruling that voided a county approval for another Google data center.

Texas reopens its permit queue for more than 1,800 stalled data-center projects

Texas has resumed approvals for more than 1,800 data-center projects that outgoing Gov. Greg Abbott’s office put on hold in August, when a moratorium covered 474 gigawatts of new power requests, Chip Dispatch reported on November 27, 2026. Chip Dispatch stresses that the 474 GW figure counts power applications, not operating computing capacity. The Financial Times reported that builders are racing to secure approvals before Democrat Gina Hinojosa takes office in January, after she pledged to rein in new data-center construction. Some developers are pursuing their own gas-turbine power to reduce reliance on the grid. No processing schedule or project list accompanied the directive.

PrismML’s Ternary Bonsai 2.5 packs a 27B coding model into about 5.9 GB of open weights

PrismML introduced Ternary Bonsai 2.5 27B on November 24, an upgrade to its compressed open-weight language model, Open Model Weights reported on November 26, 2026. Built on Qwen3.8-27B, it keeps a ternary representation: the GGUF model card lists an effective 1.76 bits per weight and a 5.93 GB packing, against 53.8 GB for the FP16 reference. PrismML reports SWE-bench Verified improving from 60.8 to 75.6 and AppWorld from 51.2 to 90.5 versus Bonsai 2 — publisher evaluations, not independent measurements. The weights are released under Apache 2.0 with GGUF and MLX options; the most compact packing needs PrismML’s llama.cpp fork, while a Q2_0 version runs on upstream llama.cpp, and optional multi-token prediction variants add a draft head for speculative decoding. Open Model Weights cautions that weight file size is not total runtime memory, since context length and cache also count.

Aleph Alpha’s Kolibri 1.1 says it cuts GPU time per task about 3.4-fold for German and English work

German developer Aleph Alpha released Kolibri 1.1 on November 23, roughly seven weeks after the first Kolibri, Open Model Weights reported on November 26, 2026. The open-weight mixture-of-experts reasoning model has roughly 75 billion total parameters and around 3.4 billion active per token, adds sparse attention, and is released under Apache 2.0. Aleph Alpha reports a roughly 3.4-fold reduction in GPU time per task versus the original Kolibri and 41% fewer generated output tokens on average; its technical report lists an aggregate score of 73.2 and 0.208 GPU-hours per 1,000 evaluation tasks. Open Model Weights notes these are publisher results, not independently reproduced benchmarks, and that a model license alone does not guarantee compliance with European privacy rules. It calls the focus on efficiency per completed task, rather than raw token speed, the release’s most interesting feature.

MemTensor opens MemT-1.0-Pro, a 554B-parameter model that builds memory into the model itself

MemTensor announced MemT-1.0-Pro on November 23, a memory-native model developed in collaboration with Nvidia and built on Nemotron 3 Ultra, Open Model Weights reported on November 25, 2026. Instead of relying only on an external store of past conversations, it tries to make memory part of the model’s computation. The publisher reports about 554 billion parameters overall and 59 billion active, plus 4.23 billion trainable memory parameters and a 13-million-parameter dynamic memory state. The checkpoint is listed on Hugging Face under the OpenMDW-1.1 license, while the project’s SGLang serving code carries a PolyForm Noncommercial license, so access to the weights is not the same as unrestricted commercial deployment. Open Model Weights says the approach raises new questions for long-running agents, such as how stored facts are updated, audited or removed, and that the benchmark results remain publisher-reported.

Europe’s largest satellite factory opens in Belgium, built to turn out up to 500 satellites a year

The largest satellite factory in Europe was inaugurated in Belgium this week by King Philippe, boosting the continent’s push to challenge Elon Musk’s Starlink, CNBC reported on November 26, 2026. Belgian manufacturer Aerospacelab, which will be renamed Ectro from Nov. 30, won a €2.4 billion ($2.6 billion) contract in September to build 264 of the 348 satellites planned for IRIS², the European Union’s secure-connectivity constellation. Employees are expected to move in in January, with production slated to begin by the end of 2028, according to AFP. SpaceX says it has more than 11,000 Starlink satellites in low Earth orbit; IRIS² aims for first launches by the end of 2029.

For this New York restaurant owner, Thanksgiving comes every Wednesday

Every Wednesday night for the past five years, Ali Riza Dogan, owner of Ali Baba Mediterranean & Turkish Cuisine in Midtown Manhattan, has loaded catering pans with hot food and driven to Chinatown to serve free meals to 100 to 150 people who are homeless or struggling, Good News Network reported on November 26, 2026. On Thanksgiving eve he brought plenty of turkey, joined by community volunteers. The routine traces back to a night years ago when, newly arrived from Turkey and working as a dishwasher, he got lost, spoke little English and spent a cold night hungry in a hallway. “We never forget where we come from,” he said. When it turns cold, he leaves the heat running in his restaurant’s entryway with a sign welcoming anyone who needs a warm place to rest. “Maybe we cannot change the whole world, but if we can help even one person sleep warmer, then we have done something meaningful.”
