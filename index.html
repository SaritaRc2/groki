Anthropic’s updated IPO filing shows almost $17 billion in quarterly revenue, and a swing back to an operating loss

Anthropic filed an amended S-1 with the SEC on Monday that adds its third quarter, CNBC reported. Revenue for the three months to September 30 was about $17 billion, up roughly 1,127% from a year earlier, but growth slowed to about 47% from the second quarter, after 144% in Q2. The company posted a $567 million operating loss after a $974 million operating profit in Q2, which the filing blames mainly on $5 billion of pricier short-term compute, according to Business Insider. Anthropic also listed about $895.4 billion in purchase commitments and leases, “substantially all” of it for compute and infrastructure, as it prepares its roadshow to list on Nasdaq as “ANT.”

Marvell agrees to buy laser maker Lumentum in a $115 billion all-stock deal

Marvell Technology will acquire Lumentum, which makes the lasers and optical parts that move data through AI data centers as light, the companies announced Monday. Lumentum holders get 4.5 Marvell shares for each share, an implied enterprise value of $115 billion and a premium of about 23.9% to the October 28 close; Marvell holders will own about 67% of the combined company. TechCrunch called it one of the biggest acquisitions in tech history. The deal still needs shareholder and regulatory approval and is expected to close in the first half of 2028.

AI News

The New York Times sues Brave, saying its search API sells scraped articles to AI companies

The New York Times and, in a separate suit, the New York Daily News, Chicago Tribune, and Orlando Sentinel have sued Brave Software, the company behind the Brave browser, The Verge reported. The complaints say Brave scraped their sites, let users get around paywalls, and sold paid access to its index to AI companies including Anthropic, Perplexity, Mistral, xAI, and Cohere, for training and answering queries. A Times spokesperson said Brave “ignored repeated requests” to stop. Perplexity disputed the suit; Brave and the other AI companies didn’t immediately comment.

Microsoft’s AI chief: today’s AI isn’t conscious, and if that changes, stop and tell everyone

Microsoft AI chief Mustafa Suleyman said in a November 1 post on X that “AIs are not conscious. They do not feel, experience, or suffer,” Business Today reported. He called building conscious AI “reckless” and said that if consciousness started to emerge in training, he would call for everyone to stop AI development until those properties could be designed out, and that any evidence should be disclosed publicly at once. He said welfare concerns should focus on people, animals, and the natural world.

Meituan’s LongCat-2.5 arrives: a 1.6-trillion-parameter agent model trained on Chinese chips

Meituan’s LongCat team released LongCat-2.5, a native multimodal model built for long coding and agent tasks, with 1.6 trillion total parameters and about 48 billion active per token. The team says it reads images as tokens in the same space as text, was trained on domestic AI accelerators, and runs 4-bit inference that boosted throughput about 43% in its own tests. Its benchmark table shows 88.4 on Terminal Bench v2.1, up from 70.8 for LongCat-2.0; these are Meituan’s numbers. It’s available through LongCat’s chat and API, and the weights haven’t been released.

OpenAI’s GPT-6.1 Terra lands on Amazon Bedrock at half the price of its bigger sibling

GPT-6.1 Terra, the everyday-workload model in OpenAI’s GPT-6 family, is now generally available on Amazon Bedrock, AWS said in its weekly roundup. OpenAI says Terra scored 15 percentage points higher than GPT-5.6 Terra on its Agents’ Last Exam evaluation, matched GPT-6 Astra, and finished tasks more than three times faster; AWS notes those are OpenAI’s figures. Input and output tokens cost at least 50% less than GPT-6.1 Sol, and it takes up to 1 million input tokens of text and images.

OpenAI’s Grid Protectors program: AI campuses that power down so homes keep the lights on

OpenAI launched its Grid Protectors Program on Monday, a national effort to design its future compute campuses to cut electricity use when the grid is under strain, and in some places to feed power back from on-site generation or batteries. The pilot is its planned campus in Effingham County, Georgia, where its agreement with Georgia Power includes up to 1,000 megawatts of flexible demand, about the average use of 700,000 households, OpenAI says. OpenAI also said it will pay the full infrastructure and electric-service costs for that campus.

Innovation

A humanoid learns to walk, grab, and deliver in one go, without ever being shown the whole task

Researchers at Seoul National University built RISE, a vision-language-action system that learns walking and object handling from separately recorded demonstrations and then strings them together into multistep tasks, TechXplore reported. On a real Unitree G1 humanoid with 43 controllable joints, RISE completed tasks of up to five stages 50% of the time, against 30% for a baseline built on Nvidia’s GR00T; in simulation it hit 87.3%. The team says the approach could cut the cost of collecting robot training data for jobs like moving goods in warehouses.

‘Teach children how to control technology’: a London teacher makes the case for AI literacy

Kasia Suliga Wasielewska, a teacher at St Joseph’s Primary School in north London, has pupils fact-check chatbot answers, chat with a Shakespeare bot, and learn how machine learning works with Micro:bit’s CreateAI tool, Metro reported. “AI must support, not replace, the teacher’s expertise,” she said.
