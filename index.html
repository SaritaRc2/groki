AI NewsLead

Hackers raid OpenCode Console’s stored API keys, and the AI models its team turned to for defense refused to look at the malware

OpenCode, the open-source AI coding agent gaining ground as an alternative to Claude Code and Cursor, said attackers broke into an older system overnight and affected 1.4% of users of OpenCode Console, its online platform where some users keep the API keys they use for AI services. Developers posted OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google spend alerts firing overnight, with charges in the hundreds of dollars on individual accounts and a few totals above $1,000, TechCrunch reports. Anthropic and Google blocked the keys they believed were exposed; OpenAI worked with OpenCode to restore access and refund credits used in the attack, and OpenCode says refunds for unauthorized charges are underway. The open-source app and command-line tool were not affected, co-founder Dax Raad said. The harder lesson came mid-fight: Raad said attackers kept trying to get in for 48 hours, and the AI models his team used for analysis started refusing when the malicious code they were asked to examine tripped safety filters, until OpenCode got access to OpenAI’s Daybreak Blue program for vetted defensive security work. OpenCode has told affected users to revoke and replace their keys with each provider and says the incident remains ongoing.

AI News

A day after the U.S. removal order, DeepSeek open-sources DeepInfer, the engine that runs its models in production

DeepSeek released DeepInfer on Tuesday, October 20, publishing on GitHub under the Apache 2.0 license the inference software it uses to serve its models at scale. For now it supports only DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and runs on Nvidia SM100 data-center GPUs and Huawei Ascend 950 chips, with optimized kernels, work split across many chips, speculative decoding, and structured outputs, The Next Web reports. It is not a complete service: the KV cache system, the split between prompt processing and answer generation, and load balancing across replicas are left out. DeepSeek says DeepInfer was first built mainly on vLLM and drew on SGLang and TensorRT-LLM; its speed figures are its own and have not been independently tested. The release came a day after U.S. civilian federal agencies were ordered to remove DeepSeek products, and the repository passed 8,000 GitHub stars by Tuesday afternoon.

AI News

OpenAI buys Orchestra Bio to put drug-development agents beside its Rosalind life-sciences models

OpenAI said on Tuesday, October 20, that it has acquired Orchestra Bio, a platform where people and AI agents work together to coordinate drug programs, and is bringing its team and technology together with Rosalind, OpenAI’s models and tools for life-sciences research. Scientists and program leaders use Orchestra’s agents to monitor and track studies; when something breaks, such as a failed manufacturing batch that could delay a clinical trial, the agents identify which activities and timelines are affected and compare ways to respond, with people making the decision. OpenAI says new medicines take roughly 10 to 15 years from target discovery to U.S. approval, and that it wants agents that connect scientific findings to the studies and next steps needed to advance them. Terms were not disclosed.

Models

Mistral’s Voxtral 3 listens in 50+ languages, talks back with emotion, and runs a full voice agent in under a second a turn

Mistral released Voxtral 3 on Tuesday, October 20: new speech-to-text and text-to-speech models plus a voice agent SDK, built on LiveKit, that streams listening, reasoning, tool calls, and speech in one pipeline so each turn completes in under a second and the agent stops talking when the caller cuts in. Voxtral Small Transcribe 3 covers 50 languages with diarization and keyphrase biasing, and Mistral says it beats Whisper, Scribe v2, and Gemini 3.5 Transcribe; the 4B Mini costs $0.0035 a minute, and a realtime version runs under 500 milliseconds. Voxtral Mini TTS 3 adjusts emotion, pace, and emphasis across 35+ languages and 50+ dialects for $13 per million characters, and Pro Voice can adapt a speaker’s voice from 30 minutes of audio. It is available through Mistral’s API or for self-deployment.

Models

NVIDIA’s new open Nemotron 3.5 Embed, Rerank, and Parse models help agents find the right page in fewer tokens

NVIDIA released open, commercially available retrieval models on Wednesday, October 21: Nemotron 3.5 Embed in 8B and 1B sizes, Nemotron 3.5 Rerank 8B, and the 631M-parameter Nemotron 3.5 Parse. Embed searches document text and images such as tables, charts, and schematics; the 8B model scored 61.17 nDCG@10 on ViDoRe v3.1, first among dense retrieval models, and the distilled 1B keeps 95.4% of that. In NVIDIA’s agent tests, adding the reranker cut agent token cost per query by about 14% while improving retrieval accuracy. Parse turns page images into structured Markdown and tables and ranks first on ParseBench among models under 1 billion parameters, at 10.5 pages per second on one H100. Weights are on Hugging Face.

Models

Bria’s Fibo 2.0 generates and edits images in one open 8B model, and tops the open-weights editing arena

Bria released Fibo 2.0, an 8B-parameter open-weights model that generates and edits images with one set of weights, plus a distilled four-step Edit Turbo checkpoint, ComfyUI Wiki reports. The model card cites an Artificial Analysis snapshot dated October 20 ranking it first among open-weights models in the image-editing arena, with an Elo of 1087, and second for text-to-image. New in 2.0: instruction edits that keep untouched regions intact, masked edits, and multi-reference edits with up to four images for cases like virtual try-on. It reads long structured JSON prompts rather than a sentence. The weights are gated on Hugging Face behind Bria’s terms, and core ComfyUI support is still pending.

Innovation

Google open-sources TorchTPU, so PyTorch code runs natively on its TPUs with the tools developers already use

Google said at PyTorch Conference 2026 on Tuesday, October 20, that TorchTPU, its native PyTorch stack for TPUs, is now open source on GitHub, making TPUs a native PyTorch option alongside GPUs and CPUs. Co-developed with Meta’s PyTorch team, it lets developers debug in eager mode and switch to torch.compile for production, and it plugs into vLLM, SGLang, llm-d, TorchTitan, Ray, and Hugging Face, where Transformers v5 can target TPUs with device_map="tpu". Google reports up to 52% model FLOPs utilization training Qwen 3 8B with near-linear scaling across up to 8,192 Ironwood TPUs. It arrives a day after Amazon’s TorchNeuron did the same for Trainium.

Innovation

Mastercard opens Agent Pay for Machines to developers, so AI agents can pay for services within a set budget

Mastercard opened Early Access to Agent Pay for Machines on Tuesday, October 20, giving developers the first version of its buyer- and seller-side APIs, a toolkit, sandbox, and documentation. Built on x402, an open protocol for requesting payment online, it works like this: a business gives an agent a job and a spending limit; when a paid service answers “402 Payment Required,” the agent signs a digital voucher within that limit, the provider verifies it and delivers, and vouchers are settled later. In a pilot demo at Cloudflare Connect, an agent for the marketing agency Hype bought social-media research from AIsa and eight campaign images through Cloudflare: nine payments, two merchants, one approved budget, and no separate accounts or API keys. It is still a pilot, and Mastercard is asking developers for feedback.

Inside Prometheus: Meta’s Ohio supercluster trained Muse and is headed toward about a million GPUs

Meta on October 19 detailed Prometheus, the gigawatt-scale supercluster at its New Albany, Ohio, data center, AI Affairs reports. Its completed first phase, already hundreds of thousands of GPUs across several buildings, trained the Muse family, including Muse Wonder, and Meta is building toward roughly 1 million GPUs meant to work as one training system. Meta reports more than 85% goodput on Muse Wonder pre-training and more than 90% on reinforcement learning, meaning that share of time went into productive training, and a mean time between interruptions of about 47,000 GPU hours, raised past 54,000 after firmware fixes. Two years ago the site was handling ordinary web traffic.

Forty-eight hours under attack: when your tools stop helping, find better ones and keep going

OpenCode’s team spent two days fending off nonstop break-in attempts, co-founder Dax Raad said, with AI defensive agents running around the clock. Then the models they leaned on for analysis started refusing, because the malicious code tripped safety filters. They did not quit; they got vetted access to OpenAI’s Daybreak Blue defensive-security program, and Raad said it improved their investigation, TechCrunch reports. The lesson for any hard week: when a tool stops working, that is a problem to solve, not a reason to stop. Name what is blocking you, ask for the access or help you need, and keep the work moving.
