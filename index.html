Meta’s AI agent Muse is mixing up conversations and may be exposing private data, report says

Meta employees have raised concerns that the company’s AI agent Muse has been mixing up conversations across different accounts and exposing private user data, TechCrunch reported on November 19, 2026, citing a Reuters report based on internal company posts. Employees said Muse surfaced childcare requests they never made, product orders they never placed and bank balances that weren’t theirs. In one case, Muse sent an unprompted message in a chat about weather and tides listing someone’s Capital One and Charles Schwab balances. Another employee said Muse reminded her to answer her sister on Signal. “This seems like an extreme privacy violation. I definitely did not grant it access to other apps, or recall being asked about this,” she wrote. “Employee testing is core to the product development process and the entire point is to get feedback,” a Meta spokesperson said, adding that employees use different versions of Muse than the public. Muse, launched in September, has more than 10 million downloads.

A wave of tech outages looks like an ‘AI-native Y2K,’ security veterans say

In the past two weeks, Apple Pay went down, the FAA grounded all flights for hours, and Microsoft suffered an outage that took down Outlook, Teams and Copilot for nearly two days, TechCrunch reported on November 19, 2026. Many of the companies have said the outages were technical issues, not cyberattacks. Longtime cybersecurity executive Dave DeWalt told TechCrunch the outages are likely the “growing pains of a security system that’s getting stronger, not weaker,” because “AI is surfacing vulnerabilities at a speed and scale we’ve never had before.” As companies rush to fix those flaws, “that surge in patching is disrupting things in the short term,” he said. Corsha founder Anusha Iyer pointed to rushed mixing of legacy and modern systems: “It’s not rapid adoption of AI yet causing these outages,” she said. DeWalt sees opportunity ahead “for the companies that help organizations move at AI speed without breaking things.”

Nvidia’s record quarter rests partly on $118 billion it guarantees for its own customers

Nvidia reported $115.8 billion in third-quarter revenue, up 103% from a year earlier and $7.8 billion above its own guidance, and guided $130 billion for the fourth quarter, Chip Dispatch reported on November 20, 2026, in a piece based on a Seeking Alpha analysis. But a growing share of that demand is financed by Nvidia itself: the company has extended $118 billion in guarantees and holds $109.7 billion in equity stakes, according to the analysis. The guarantees equal 33% of trailing revenue, compared with Nortel’s 19% vendor-financing ratio in 2000, and some 89% of them sit on a single campus leased to OpenAI. The Federal Reserve flagged the pattern as “circular financing” in November, the piece notes. The analyst rates Nvidia a Sell on a 12-month view and holds no position. Chip Dispatch says the open question is how much of the $130 billion guide stands on Nvidia’s own balance sheet.

Fireworks AI says open models now trail the frontier by about three months

On the day Anthropic went public, the talk at the Slush startup conference in Helsinki was open-weight models, TechCrunch reported on November 19, 2026, in an onstage interview with Fireworks AI co-founder Chenyu Zhao. Fireworks runs open models for businesses and helps them tune them on their own data. Asked how far behind open models are, Zhao said: “These days it’s at most six months behind, probably closer to three.” He predicted “a two-model world, with frontier and open models in parallel,” adding, “you don’t need a frontier model for basic arithmetic.” Once companies are spending $10,000 to $100,000 a month on tokens, he said, “it’s time to think about open source.” TechCrunch notes Fireworks disclosed two security incidents this fall, and Notion has disconnected from it. Zhao said there is “no indication” its zero-retention promise for inference traffic was compromised.

Cloudflare releases open weights for its Clairic document-extraction models

Cloudflare has moved its Clairic extraction models from a hosted preview into open weights, Open Model Weights reported on November 20, 2026. On November 18, the company announced downloadable weights for a redesigned Clairic model, about 27 billion parameters, and a new, smaller Clairic-flash, about 9 billion. Unlike a chatbot, Clairic is built to return specified typed fields from unstructured inputs such as receipts, commercial documents and support material, with confidence scores and character spans for checking. The updated model now reads PDFs, so it can pull related fields from across pages, up to a combined four images or PDF pages per request. Cloudflare’s own tests list median response times of 358 milliseconds for Clairic and 155 milliseconds for Clairic-flash; the outlet notes these are internal figures it has not reproduced, and public extraction benchmarks are not yet available. Both are also hosted on Workers AI.

Nvidia reportedly nears a $500 million-plus deal for diffusion-LLM startup Inception

Nvidia is nearing a deal to acquire Inception, a Palo Alto startup building diffusion-based language models, for more than $500 million, Chip Dispatch reported on November 20, 2026, citing The Information. Neither company has commented, and no second outlet has confirmed the talks. Most language models write one token at a time; diffusion models start from a rough draft of the whole output and refine it over several passes, letting them process many tokens in parallel. Inception launched its Mercury model in February 2025, claiming “up to 10x faster inference speeds and 10x lower inference costs” than conventional models, and founder Stefano Ermon has said the models have been benchmarked at more than 1,000 tokens per second. Secondary summaries said the technology would feed Nvidia’s open Nemotron models. It would be one of Nvidia’s first reported deals since Jensen Huang resigned as CEO.

Voyager 1 passes one light-day from Earth, and the Empire State Building glows gold

The Empire State Building was lit gold this week to celebrate NASA’s Voyager 1 passing one light-day from Earth, Good News Network reported on November 19, 2026. On Wednesday the spacecraft reached the distance light travels in 24 hours, about 16 billion miles, so a signal now takes a full day to get home. “The color gold is emblematic of the gold-plated copper phonograph record that is flying on Voyager,” NASA wrote. The record carries 115 images, natural sounds, music and spoken greetings in 55 languages. Launched in 1977, Voyager 1 crossed into interstellar space in 2012. True to form, it marked the occasion with a hiccup: on Tuesday it stopped sending science and engineering data, though its carrier signal was still found. “We never know for sure what’s going to happen with the Voyagers, but it constantly amazes me when they just keep going,” project manager Suzanne Dodd told CNN in 2024.

She checked on her ‘loud’ upstairs neighbor, and strangers raised more than $109,000

When Heidi Widmer moved to Culver City, she was losing sleep over a thumping upstairs neighbor, Good News Network reported on November 19, 2026. Then she met Carole, an 87-year-old with Parkinson’s disease who had taken about 20 minutes to get down the stairs with a walker. “Am I too loud for you?” Carole asked. “That was just such a humbling moment for me,” Widmer said.
