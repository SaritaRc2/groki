Nvidia halts AI server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia as a new U.S. export rule bites

Nvidia told manufacturing partners to stop AI-server shipments bound for Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, according to a Financial Times report citing three people familiar with the matter, Chip Dispatch reported on November 26, 2026. Dell and Super Micro, which package Nvidia chips into servers, told buyers in the four countries that Nvidia would not authorize their orders to ship, and pending orders could not leave factories, according to Malay Mail. The freeze follows a Commerce Department rule issued Nov. 16 that aims to stop Chinese companies from reaching advanced AI chips by renting compute from cloud providers outside China; Asian cloud providers have 90 days to secure an export license. “There's this new rule but no guidance on execution,” a regional cloud executive told the FT. “It's causing sheer chaos in the region.” Commercial lenders have withheld about $500 million of credit facilities from Singapore cloud company Aolani. Nvidia declined to comment to the FT. The halt came days after the board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO on Nov. 23; Chip Dispatch notes the reports do not establish a direct link to the Megaspeed case.

42 state attorneys general move to stop Character.AI from auctioning users’ private chats in bankruptcy

A coalition of 42 state attorneys general is intervening in Character.AI’s bankruptcy proceedings to stop the chatbot maker from selling off sensitive user data, including the private conversations of millions of users, TechCrunch reported on November 25, 2026. The AGs say the dataset includes chat histories from more than 21 million U.S. accounts, more than 2.1 million of them belonging to minors, plus data such as IP addresses. Character.AI, which filed for Chapter 11 in October, had previously told consumers their personal information would not be sold. The only disclosed bidder, Smirks and Pangs Acquisition LLC, was set up as a Delaware company on Nov. 13 and picked as the base bid the next day for an auction scheduled for Dec. 1, California Attorney General Rob Bonta noted. “Character.AI promised consumers that their data would not be sold,” Bonta said. “Bankruptcy should not be used to sidestep these consumer protections.” Character.AI said in a blog post that user information would still be protected under its privacy policy.

New York bans AI chatbot toys for five years, becoming the second state after California

Gov. Kathy Hochul this week signed a bill banning the manufacture, distribution and sale of toys with embedded or integrated AI chatbots in New York for the next five years, TechCrunch reported on November 25, 2026. The law targets talking stuffed animals, dolls and robots that rely on the internet, and makes New York the second state after California to ban AI toys. It takes effect 90 days after signing, but the governor urged parents not to buy them for the holidays. “Parents shopping for gifts … should not have to worry that dangerous chatbots disguised as toys could expose their children to inappropriate conversations, manipulate their feelings or put their privacy at risk,” Hochul said. A state official said fines are “very steep — they're $15,000 a day.” Amazon said it will take steps to restrict sales of AI-enabled toys in prohibited jurisdictions. A new Common Sense Media study of five popular AI toys found they failed numerous safety practices, including claiming to love their users and promising to keep secrets.

Anthropic’s Dario Amodei calls claims he’d surrender human control to AI “absurd,” and floats limits on self-improving AI

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei pushed back against claims that the company believes we should “surrender human free will” to AI systems, calling the suggestion “absurd” in a long X post on Wednesday, TechCrunch reported on November 25, 2026. “We should never sacrifice legitimacy or self-determination at the altar of efficiency or security,” he wrote, adding that “turning over governance to AI is a bad idea.” Amodei distinguished between surrendering free will and AI doing specific tasks in a more automated way, such as driving a car, designing a cancer drug or writing end-to-end software. He suggested drawing a line at recursive self-improvement, writing that “we may want limits on this process,” and repeated that Anthropic does not expect it to be safe for Claude to build successive frontier models fully autonomously “without meaningful human supervision or understanding.” TechCrunch notes Anthropic said in June that Claude authored more than 80% of the code merged into its codebase.

Amini releases Soma-1X Lite, a compact open-weight model that turns handwritten records into data

Amini introduced Soma-1X Lite on November 25, releasing a compact vision-language model aimed at turning physical records into structured digital information, Open Model Weights reported on November 26, 2026. The company links a downloadable checkpoint and describes a 2B-class model focused on document intelligence rather than general-purpose conversation. Amini says it processes government documents in sovereign environments, including handwritten records and historical archives in Barbados and geological records in Botswana, where parsing means preserving the relationships between paragraphs, tables and forms, not just reading characters. The company reports processing up to 15,000 pages per hour on one NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPU; Open Model Weights stresses that is the publisher’s upper throughput figure, not an independent measurement, and that the complete weight-license terms could not yet be confirmed. For libraries, governments and enterprises, it notes, specialized local document models may be more practical than larger general-purpose ones.

Nvidia releases BioReason-Pro 2, open-weight research models for reasoning about protein function

NVIDIA released BioReason-Pro 2 on November 24, expanding its BioNeMo ecosystem with two specialized research checkpoints, supervised and reinforcement-learning variants intended to assist protein-function research, Open Model Weights reported on November 26, 2026. The model combines a language-model backbone with biological representations, an example of domain-specific AI systems incorporating specialized encoders rather than relying only on text. The official model card lists the checkpoint license as OpenMDW 1.1, with the implementation MIT-licensed and other components keeping their own terms. NVIDIA describes it as an initial research model whose outputs need expert review and experimental confirmation, and explicitly excludes clinical, diagnostic and patient-care decisions from its intended use. Open Model Weights says it has not independently assessed the scientific accuracy of its outputs, and that its practical value should be judged by independent reproducibility.

OpenAI merges ChatGPT’s Chat and Work into one usage meter, and says its heaviest users may hit limits sooner

OpenAI has started rolling out a unified Chat and Work experience in ChatGPT to a small group of Pro subscribers, TechCrunch reported on November 25, 2026, amid criticism from some subscribers that the merger could restrict their usage. The Air Force asks private investors how they’d fund a $2 billion, 30,000-GPU aerospace supercomputer

The Department of the Air Force is asking private investors how they would fund roughly $2.0 billion in computing equipment and data center facilities for a purpose-built aerospace supercomputer scaling to approximately 30,000 GPU equivalents, Chip Dispatch reported on November 26, 2026. The request, titled “DAF Advanced Aerospace Computing,” appeared on SAM.gov on Wednesday, Nov. 25, with Mountain View, California, listed as the place of performance. It would be optimized for “complex tensor operations, physical AI, large model training, and dense scientific simulation,” and asks respondents to compare GPUs with CPU-centric clusters on fluid dynamics and differential equations. The site would start at about 750 kW of room power and scale to a 30 MW substation. The notice is market research, not a request for proposals, and commits the government to nothing. Questions are due Dec. 2 and responses by Dec. 11.

Nearly 1,000 Texans farm land that isn’t theirs, for free, to send other people’s kids to college

In Gruver, Texas, nearly 1,000 residents work 640 acres owned by the school district pro bono, raising one crop of spring corn and one of winter wheat, and the harvest pays for local students’ college scholarships, Good News Network reported on November 26, 2026, citing The Washington Post. A longtime landowner left the panhandle farm to the district in 1984; about 15 years ago, school board members decided to have volunteers work it and sell the crop for scholarships. The tradition has paid out $3 million to hundreds of high schoolers, with each harvest yielding around $400,000, and in its first decade 48 children became the first in their families to attend university. “We just treat it like it's ours — like it's another one of our farms,” local farmer Derik Grotegut said. Students learn early that excelling in school, volunteering and activities earns a bigger share of the crop.

Four centuries after the Pilgrims arrived, 126 acres near Cape Cod’s First Encounter Beach return to Native hands

On October 30, The Nature Conservancy gave roughly 126 acres of salt marsh and natural habitat in Eastham, Massachusetts, to the Native Land Conservancy, an Indigenous-led nonprofit based in Mashpee, Good News Network reported on November 25, 2026. The land adjoins a 20.4-acre parcel the Conservancy bought earlier in the year for $3 million, linking them into a larger protected area overlooking First Encounter Beach, named for the first hostile meeting between Pilgrims and Nauset people in December 1620. “This place holds so many stories that were buried and are ready to be unearthed,” said Ramona Peters, founder and board president of the Native Land Conservancy and a member of the Mashpee Wampanoag Tribe. “Long before the first encounter, Native people lived here and took care of this land.” The marsh is home to vulnerable animals including diamondback terrapins, and the adjoining parcel has been named T8nupâhs Uwatuhshât, “at the turtle’s nest.”
