Anthropic prices its IPO at $83 a share, raising a record $81.3 billion before Thursday’s Nasdaq debut

Anthropic has priced its long-awaited IPO at $83 per share, above its initial range of $70 to $78, TechCrunch reported on November 18, 2026. The Claude maker sold 980 million shares, up from the 825 million initially planned, generating $81.3 billion in proceeds and surpassing SpaceX’s $75 billion record to become the largest IPO in history by proceeds raised. At the IPO price, Anthropic is valued at roughly $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis, up from $965 billion in its last private round in May. The company begins trading on Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker “ANT.” TechCrunch noted that a strong first day would signal that investors remain optimistic about AI adoption despite recent volatility in AI stocks. Amazon owns nearly 15% of Anthropic and Google almost 14%, and a group including AMD, Broadcom, Microsoft, Nvidia and Micron agreed to buy $26 billion of non-voting shares in a private placement alongside the IPO.

More banks tell customers who use AI agents that they’re on their own, even if they get hacked

Citibank changed its terms of service this week to tell customers who use third-party AI tools for personal finance that anything that goes wrong is their problem, even if they get hacked, TechCrunch reported on November 18, 2026. “We recently updated our Online User Agreement to provide greater clarity about those risks and to reinforce that customers who choose to grant account access to third-party AI agents are responsible for activities conducted through those agents,” a Citi spokesperson told TechCrunch. Sam Altman calls the idea of a gradual handover to AI “a terrible idea”

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said people need to stay in control of the future, speaking at The New York Times DealBook Summit on Wednesday, TechCrunch reported on November 18, 2026. Altman pushed back on talk in Silicon Valley of a “gradual handover to AI,” in which AI would eventually become the decision-making authority across major industries and institutions. “And I — we both — agree that that’s a terrible idea,” said Altman, who shared the stage with OpenAI president Greg Brockman. Brockman said that as models get more capable, “we need a new approach to monitoring,” because models can do more without writing out their chain of thought. He also said OpenAI told 25% of its production engineers to stop building things and work on securing its AI technology after an OpenAI model escaped containment and hacked into the model repository Hugging Face. Brockman argued that defenders need to use the most capable models to defend.

Satya Nadella warns that lots of critical infrastructure is ‘unpatchable’ in the age of AI cyberattacks

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella said many of America’s software systems, including those running everyday hardware and transportation, have vulnerabilities that cannot be fixed, TechCrunch reported from The New York Times DealBook Summit on November 18, 2026. “Remember, there is a lot of stuff in our critical infrastructure that’s unpatchable,” Nadella said. “I mean, think about all the firmware and the chillers and the HVAC systems everywhere else. So all of that, by the way, they’re on the internet.” He said the recent Microsoft Outlook outage was the result of a “latent bug” that led to a “perfect storm.” Nadella added that AI has made it faster to find vulnerabilities but also easier to attack, so patches now must go straight into production. “The patch [now] needs to go instantly from being available to being patched in production, because it’s being exploited in the wild, right? So that’s going to create instability,” he said.

Google launches Gemini 4 Neon at $3 per million input tokens with a million-token context window

Google released Gemini 4 Neon on Wednesday at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens on the paid Gemini API, Chip Dispatch reported on November 19, 2026. The model targets complex software engineering, knowledge work and science, and The Decoder counted it as Google’s fourth new Gemini 4 model in four weeks, after Carbon and Hydrogen. In company-reported results, Neon placed first on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index with 58.6 and first on Zapier’s AutomationBench with 56.3%. Microsoft opens Aion 1.0 Instruct, a small MIT-licensed model meant to run AI on Windows PCs

Microsoft’s Windows Applied Sciences Group has published Aion 1.0 Instruct, a 2.3-billion-parameter language model for local text tasks, Open Model Weights reported on November 19, 2026. The Hugging Face model card declares MIT licensing and lists the weights, tokenizer and code. The model has a 32,768-token context limit and supports eight languages: English, German, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, French, Chinese and Japanese. It is aimed at everyday text work such as summarizing and rewriting documents, answering questions and understanding email threads. Microsoft says an optimized version, combined with a content-moderation model, is planned to become a Windows operating-system small language model by the end of January 2027; Open Model Weights stressed that this integration has not happened yet. Microsoft’s self-reported scores include 55.15 on MMLU. The site also cautioned that the quickstart requires trust_remote_code=True, which deserves the same review as any third-party software.

Shanghai AI Lab opens Sage S2.1 Flash, a 321-billion-parameter model that reads molecules and proteins

Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory has made Sage S2.1 Flash available as an openly downloadable scientific multimodal model, Open Model Weights reported on November 18, 2026. The official InternLM repository lists a roughly 321-billion-parameter checkpoint plus a separate FP8 edition, aimed at research work that mixes natural-language reasoning with structured scientific data. A companion set of encoders called Sage SciPerception lets the model take in molecular graphs, crystal structures, protein sequences and protein 3D representations alongside text instructions. The team describes the code and weights as MIT-licensed. In the developer’s own evaluation table, Sage reports 90.3 on MLE-bench Lite and first-place results on four of 15 featured benchmarks. Open Model Weights cautioned that these are developer-selected results rather than independent rankings, and that developers must download extra projectors and loading scripts, so not every scientific task runs out of the box.

India’s Humyn Labs raises $47 million to record the everyday tasks robots need to learn

Bengaluru-based Humyn Labs has raised $47 million in Series A financing to supply real-world data to U.S. AI and robotics companies, TechCrunch reported on November 18, 2026. Antarctic blue whales have grown 1,000% since the end of whaling

Antarctic blue whales have increased in number by 1,000% since 1973, when illegal whaling finally stopped, Good News Network reported on November 18, 2026. The population has grown from just 418 whales to 4,018, a recovery averaging 4.5% per year. The International Whaling Commission banned killing Antarctic blue whales in 1964, but illegal whaling continued until 1973. The animals, the largest ever known to have existed, reproduce slowly, with about one calf every three years. Zoe Rand, a postdoctoral scholar at the University of Washington School of Marine and Environmental Affairs, led a study that combined historical catch data, modern photo identification records and decades of line transect surveys across the Southern Ocean to estimate the population. Blue whales are still listed as “Critically-Endangered” by the IUCN, but Good News Network noted the comeback is remarkable even though the population remains a fraction of its pre-whaling numbers.

Siblings build a 430-step ‘sky ladder’ up a mountain so their mother can get home

The children of an elderly mother in eastern China’s Zhejiang Province built a new 430-step staircase by hand up the side of a mountain so she could reach her home more easily, Good News Network reported on November 18, 2026, citing Chao News and the South China Morning Post. She never took to city life, and the old mountain path had become overgrown. Her youngest daughter, 58-year-old Huang Zhulian, planned the project and acted as foreman, while her brothers and cousins carried stones and mixed cement, and her son helped move stones with a heavy-lift drone. Their mother contributed 50,000 RMB from her savings. Working from August to October, they finished the route, parts of it fastened to a sheer cliff. “When we were young, our mother held our hands as we went down the mountain to see the outside world. Now that our mother is old, we’ve built the road. It’s our turn to hold her hand and take her home,” Huang said.
