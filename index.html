GPT-6 reaches every ChatGPT user, and answers now come with buttons, charts, and tools

OpenAI said October 7 that it is bringing GPT-6 to the more than 1.2 billion people who use ChatGPT each week, along with a new capability it calls Intelligent UI. Instead of only text, answers can now mix in graphics, tappable buttons, forms, charts, and small interactive tools built on the spot; The Verge describes examples like a clickable diagram of a seven-speed bicycle, a retirement savings calculator, a bill splitter, and a retro game generated right inside the chat. OpenAI says GPT-6 can also start answering while it is still thinking, searches the web better, and showed stronger resistance to attempts to bypass its safety training. The rollout began with Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise in the Chat tab, powered by GPT-6 Sol, and expands to the Free and Go tiers starting today, October 8, on GPT-6 Luna. The models behind Work and Codex are not changing.

AI News

The day after: OpenAI’s 370 math results split mathematicians, and its own advisers’ rules come up short

A day after OpenAI posted 722 papers grouped into more than 370 result families, Fortune reported October 7 that the volume stunned mathematicians and divided them: some see huge new areas to explore, while others worry about the approach. OpenAI says an unreleased internal model averaged about three hours of computing time per solution, weeks after it claimed an unreleased model solved the Navier-Stokes equations, a Millennium Prize problem. New Scientist notes the release falls short of guidelines from OpenAI’s own advisory group of mathematicians, which recommends naming the model, releasing prompts and compute costs, and disclosing how many comparable problems were tried and failed. OpenAI’s Lindsay McCallum Rémy told New Scientist the company wants “to give the mathematical community time and space to assess this work.”

Samsung guides to a record $80 billion quarterly profit on AI memory demand

Samsung Electronics said October 8 it expects third-quarter operating profit of about 107.4 trillion won, roughly $80 billion, and sales of about 195 trillion won, according to its earnings guidance. Yonhap reports profit is up 782.5% from a year earlier and revenue up 126.6%, beating the average market forecast by 1.6%, and it would be Samsung’s fourth straight quarter of record revenue and profit. Models

Anthropic’s Claude Haiku 5.5 is its fastest, cheapest small model, at about 75% less to run

Anthropic released Claude Haiku 5.5 on October 7, calling it the cheapest, fastest, and most capable small model it has shipped. It is built for high-volume work such as summaries, classification, customer support, browser use, and serving as a subagent next to Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5, and it is the first Haiku with an adjustable effort setting. Anthropic lists input at $0.10 and output at $0.50 per million tokens for prompts up to 100,000 tokens, and says it costs around 75% less to run on average than Haiku 4.5. In Anthropic’s own benchmarks it scored 72.4% on an offline subset of the OSWorld 2.1 computer-use test, versus 15.7% for Haiku 4.5. Anthropic also halved Sonnet 5.5’s cache-read price and is adding monthly API credits for Max and Team subscribers.

Models

New “Humanity’s Sixth Sense” test: people score 93%, the best AI model 54%

Scale Labs and Elorian introduced Humanity’s Sixth Sense on October 7, a benchmark of 522 open-ended tasks across 288 images and 234 video clips that test the intuition people use at a glance: whether two books will fit on a shelf, why a runner slows down for a bus, who holds authority in a room. Twenty human participants scored 93.1%, while the strongest of 25 models, GPT-6-astra, reached 53.6% and the median model 30.9%. More thinking did not close the gap, and Scale says 94% of the 8,573 model failures traced to perception or inference rather than faulty logic; social understanding was the weakest area for 21 of 25 models. Even with tools to crop, zoom, and search inside Claude Code and Codex, the best setup reached 59.3% on a subset.

Motivation

Basic prompting is losing value; building with AI is climbing. That’s the next skill to learn

Workday’s October 2026 Global Workforce Report, released October 5, analyzed skills on job postings at about 550 employers and found demand for basic AI skills like simple prompting peaked in January 2026 and then fell 25%, while demand for hands-on skills such as building AI tools, automating workflows, and AI engineering climbed 51% between September 2025 and July 2026. Only 28% of business leaders expect AI to reduce headcount, against 40% who expect it to help them get more out of the people they have. In a survey of 6,000 workers, 65% said they’re confident they can learn new skills, and 76% of heavy AI users expect AI to open new career opportunities. “It’s whether they can learn fast enough to solve the next problem,” said Workday’s Phil Willburn. Today’s move: build one small thing with AI, not just ask it a question.

Friday’s Revolution and Evolution symposium brings the Smithsonian to the Hilliard

Festivals Acadiens et Créoles’ pre-festival symposium runs Friday, October 9, from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. at the Hilliard Art Museum, 710 East St. Mary Boulevard, on the theme “Revolution and Evolution: Celebrating 250 Years of Cultural Fusion on the Bayou.” The Center for Louisiana Studies, the festival, the Smithsonian, and the Hilliard put it together.
