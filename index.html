United won’t let personal AI agents book its flights, citing privacy and regulatory concerns

United says it won’t let personal AI assistants book tickets on the airline, citing privacy and regulatory concerns, The Verge reported on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, citing the Wall Street Journal. United is concerned that agents could expose customers’ information, make purchases they didn’t intend, and skirt federal rules such as having travelers attest that their bags don’t contain explosives. Agents can still show users United flights but won’t be able to complete the transaction on their behalf. “To be honest, I don’t want to create another middleman,” United CEO Scott Kirby said at an event on Tuesday, according to the Journal. United said it could reconsider as third-party agents advance, but wants to make sure it has “established protections for travelers first.”

Google DeepMind unveils AlphaGenome 2 to predict how DNA changes affect individual cell types

Google DeepMind unveiled AlphaGenome 2 on Wednesday, November 11, 2026, a successor to its AlphaGenome model designed to predict the effects of genetic variants on individual cell types and states. The model was trained on more than 100 million individual cell profiles across 68 tissues, and DeepMind says it identifies nearly twice as many variants that can change gene expression than its previous state-of-the-art model. Researchers found it could make predictions about new cell types and biological states without retraining and could flag gaps in experimental data. Genomics lead Žiga Avsec said its finer predictions are “more expensive” to produce, so it won’t replace AlphaGenome soon. Google is making AlphaGenome 2 available for non-commercial research via the AlphaGenome API, with a limited private preview for commercial research.

Runway opens up Praxis-1, its 27B world action model for robots, under Apache 2.0

Runway made Praxis-1, its world action model for robotics, available to everyone with open weights under the Apache 2.0 license on Wednesday, November 11, 2026. Praxis-1 is a 27B-parameter mixture-of-experts diffusion transformer that predicts video and actions together, built on the large-scale video pretraining behind Runway’s world models. Runway says it is the top-performing world action model on NVIDIA’s RoboLab-120 leaderboard, reaching 46.2% success across 1,200 episodes versus 42.9% for FLUX 3 Action, 36.8% for Cosmos3 Nano and 28.0% for π0.5. Fine-tuned on Google’s RT-1 robot, which never appeared in its training data, it reached 80.0% success on SimplerEnv versus 52.4% for the original RT-1 policy. Weights and inference and fine-tuning code are on GitHub and Hugging Face, including for commercial use.

Zyphra releases ZONOS3-Mini, an open voice-cloning speech model that runs in real time on phones and in the browser

Zyphra released the weights for ZONOS3-Mini, a compact text-to-speech model that performs zero-shot voice cloning from recordings up to 60 seconds long, mlllm.io reported on November 11, 2026. It can design new voices through age, accent and timbre settings and adjust emotion and delivery down to individual words, and it runs in real time in the browser and on mobile devices without a GPU server. The architecture is an 11-layer mixture-of-experts model with 64 experts, about 800 million shared and roughly 100 million active parameters. Zyphra cites time-to-first-audio under 570 ms in the browser on a Pixel 9, under 150 ms on a MacBook Air M3 and an average of 80 ms on an H100 with 8 parallel requests. The weights are under Apache 2.0, and its claim of ranking first among open TTS models rests on Zyphra’s own internal ZTTS2-Eval benchmark.

Plurai raises $13 million to make AI agents reliable with self-driving-style simulations

Plurai, a startup founded by former Nexar engineers Ilan Kadar and Elad Levi, said Wednesday, November 11, 2026, that it raised $13 million in seed funding led by Team8, with participation from U&I, Velocity and Mercer, TechCrunch reported. Plurai builds a digital twin of a company’s systems, including the knowledge base and tools an agent will use, then maps the agent’s possible flows to prepare it for edge cases, the same simulation approach used for self-driving cars. The platform works alongside agentic tools like Claude Code, Codex, Copilot and Cursor. Kadar says its bigger promise is post-training small open-weight models for individual customers, and tests with Nvidia and Nebius showed its models outperforming midtier frontier models at lower cost. “It is very easy to build a demo, but [hard] to get something that will be reliable and cost effective,” Kadar said.
