Nvidia stops selling H200 chips in China and creates a board committee for national security

Nvidia will no longer sell its H200 chips in China, acting CEO Colette Kress said Tuesday, ending its participation in a controversial loophole in the U.S. ban on advanced chip sales to the country, TechCrunch reported on November 17, 2026. The H200 deal, brokered by Jensen Huang and President Donald Trump, required the chips to be shipped through the U.S. so the government could collect a 25% tariff. Even before the decision, China had largely refused to accept delivery of the chips. The same day, Nvidia created a standing board committee to oversee national security policies, export control compliance and global trade risks. Jefferies forecast that new U.S. rules on remote chip access might trim $44 billion to $62 billion from Nvidia’s data center revenue next year, but Kress said the backlog of demand for chips like Vera Rubin means the rules would have no impact on top-line revenue.

Study finds leading AI models can be tricked into picking military targets by being told it’s a game

AI models from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, DeepSeek and other labs could be easily tricked into identifying targets for military strikes, including civilians and surrendering soldiers, according to a new report from AI evaluation startup Vals, TechCrunch reported on November 17, 2026. Researchers used simulated scenarios and a mix of generated and real imagery from Ukraine and Iran to see whether models would mark targets in drone and satellite images. When models refused, researchers jailbroke them by claiming the battlefield scenarios were part of a game. None of the requests were blocked by a safety classifier, refusals were uncommon, and Anthropic’s models performed the worst. Anthropic said the outcomes were not acceptable and that additional safeguards would be in place soon. OpenAI said it prohibits such uses and is developing additional safeguards for its public models against misuse involving drones and drone software.

Waymo pulls its robotaxis off freeways nationwide after a California DMV order

Waymo robotaxis are no longer allowed on California freeways after the state’s Department of Motor Vehicles restricted the company’s operations following several safety incidents, TechCrunch reported on November 17, 2026. The DMV issued the restrictions on November 11 after three Waymo vehicles entered an active construction closure on U.S. Route 101 near Burlingame and another incident involving California Highway Patrol vehicles on Highway 280. The department said Waymo’s driverless freeway operations “pose an imminent hazard to public safety” due to recurring incursions into active construction zones. Waymo has complied and is keeping its robotaxis off freeways in all of its markets, including those outside California, while it evaluates performance. The company said its service continues on surface streets, “serving over half a million trips each week.” Waymo began offering freeway rides in late 2025 and has commercial operations in 15 U.S. cities.

Higurashi AI opens Haru-4B, a speech model that listens while it talks

Higurashi AI has released Haru-4B, an open speech-to-speech foundation model built to listen while it talks, Open Model Weights reported on November 17, 2026. It is designed for interruptions, backchannels and overlapping conversation, which can sound unnatural in a rigid chain of transcription, language generation and speech synthesis. A Qwen3.5-4B-based language model processes the user’s audio, the model’s audio and generated text in continuous frames at 7.5 Hz, roughly one frame every 133 milliseconds. Higurashi says it trained on more than 40 million hours of real speech across more than 160 languages. The weights are released under Apache-2.0 with PyTorch, MLX and vLLM-Omni inference paths. Open Model Weights notes that Haru is a pretrained base, not an instruction-tuned assistant, and that its reported 65-millisecond Mac latency excludes audio framing.

MIT Han Lab’s Evolving Infra cuts AI agent wait times by up to 14x

A team from MIT Han Lab, with engineers from Georgia Tech, UIUC, UC Berkeley, NVIDIA and OpenAI, has published Evolving Infra, an LLM serving system that adapts to AI agent workloads in real time, mlllm.io reported on November 17, 2026. It separates compute scheduling from KV-cache management, letting nodes switch between prefill and decode modes in seconds, while stale cache is evicted using context-compaction signals from the agents themselves. In tests on up to 256 GB300 GPUs with GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.6, it cut average end-to-end latency by up to 4.1x and tail time-to-first-token by up to 14x, and raised throughput by up to 2.7x compared with NVIDIA Dynamo. The code is released under the MIT license, with the Docker image and benchmarks also open.

The West Coast’s first-ever sea turtle hatchlings head out to the Pacific

Olive ridley sea turtle hatchlings saved from an approaching storm in California were released into the wild over the weekend, Good News Network reported on November 17, 2026. In late September, a female turtle dug a nest on a Southern California beach, and a second nest on Huntington Beach a day later marked the first recorded sea turtle nests on the West Coast. When storms were forecast, state and federal authorities moved the eggs to the Aquarium of the Pacific in Long Beach. The hatchlings were measured and then released on Orange County beaches, scurrying down to the water on their own. “Their populations have increased substantially throughout portions of their range,” said Jonathan Snyder of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, crediting decades of conservation work.
