OpenAI drops 372 math results from an unreleased model, and mathematicians ask for receipts

Scientific American reported October 6 that OpenAI posted 722 manuscripts, grouped into 372 result families, to a GitHub repository at 6 p.m. EDT, each claimed to resolve or make substantial progress on a major open question in mathematics or theoretical computer science. The claims include a solution to the four-dimensional Kakeya conjecture, improvements to important computer algorithms, and progress toward the Riemann hypothesis, and many results are already verified in Lean, a language that checks a proof’s logic. An OpenAI spokesperson said the unreleased model produced almost every result from a single prompt to a single agent, though some may have taken multiple attempts; The Verge reports OpenAI says the average result used the equivalent of three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking. OpenAI did not publish prompts, only average compute, despite an advisory group of mathematicians asking for them. “We should ask for receipts,” said MIT’s Andrew Sutherland, while Toronto’s Daniel Litt called the release “a good thing for mathematics.” Mathematicians will need months to judge which proofs carry genuinely new ideas.

AI News

Meta, Walmart, Stripe, and Sierra publish an open standard for AI agents that shop for you

Quartz, citing CNBC, reported October 6 that Meta, Walmart, Stripe, and enterprise AI startup Sierra are publishing a “personal agent protocol,” an open standard for how AI agents interact with businesses online. Sierra co-founder and OpenAI chairman Bret Taylor, who leads the effort, said companies have no reliable way to tell whether an AI agent or a human is visiting: “It is kind of chaos until such a standard exists.” He compared it to signing into other sites with a Google or Facebook account; the protocol is meant to verify that a bot is acting for a real person and show businesses what agents are doing on their platforms. OpenAI and Anthropic are not on board yet, though Taylor said he expects them to join.

Marvell targets up to $90 billion in revenue by fiscal 2031 on AI data-center demand

At its October 6 investor day, Marvell set its first-ever fiscal 2031 revenue target of $70 billion to $90 billion, Quartz reports, far above the $47 billion analyst consensus CNBC cited; Marvell posted $8.2 billion in its most recent fiscal year. Mistral’s Large 4 “Le Chonk” arrives in public preview at 1.05 trillion parameters

Mistral AI announced Mistral Large 4 on October 6, Unite.AI reports, a natively multimodal mixture-of-experts model that its documentation lists at 1.05 trillion total parameters with 49 billion active, a 1.6-billion-parameter vision encoder, and a 1-million-token context window. It’s in public preview for all users through Mistral’s API as mistral-large-4, priced at $0.68 per million input tokens and $2.09 per million output tokens, with open weights planned for the end of October. Mistral says it is the best open-weights model from the United States or Europe on aggregated benchmarks, with state-of-the-art results in cyber defense, manufacturing, and finance, and that it was built end-to-end in Europe. Those claims are Mistral’s own, and no checkpoint is on Hugging Face yet.

Google’s EmbeddingGemma 2 puts text, code, images, video, and audio in one search space

Google DeepMind launched EmbeddingGemma 2 on October 6, an open 740-million-parameter model that maps text, code, images, video, and audio into one shared embedding space, the kind of index that powers search and retrieval. Built on the Gemma 4 architecture and released under the Apache 2.0 license, it is small enough for on-device use. Google’s examples: finding a specific video clip from a voice memo, or searching hours of audio recordings with a text query, all with a single model. Google says the original text-only EmbeddingGemma has passed 20 million downloads.

Innovation

Reka’s Rho-1 makes video and drives a robot from the same 19B-parameter weights

Reka AI released Rho-1 on October 5 as a research preview, AIvio reports: a 19-billion-parameter model that takes in and produces text, images, video, and robot actions inside one network. The same weights that predict the next video frame also produce the next robot action. To get around scarce robot training data, Reka uses a model that recovers control signals from ordinary internet video. It was trained on 320 Nvidia H100 GPUs over about three months. Reka is candid about limits: structure drifts past about 30 seconds, objects get lost across a video, and native resolution is 672 by 384. No weights are released, and none of the figures has been checked outside Reka.

Google funds 890 megawatts of new nuclear power by upgrading 11 existing reactors

Google and Constellation Energy announced on October 6 a 20-year power purchase agreement to add 890 megawatts of new nuclear capacity to the PJM grid by upgrading equipment at 11 Constellation reactors in Illinois, Pennsylvania, and New Jersey, rather than building a new plant. Constellation says it represents more than $4.3 billion of new investment, about 7,200 construction jobs, and roughly the output of a large new reactor. The first upgrade is expected by 2028, and Google says the full 890 megawatts arrives before the end of 2032. Google also signed a 15-year supply deal for another 2,700 megawatts and will deploy Gemini Enterprise to help Constellation run its plants.

Motivation

Forty-five minutes of learning how to question AI nearly doubled students’ fixes

Scienmag reported October 6 on a randomized trial in the International Journal of STEM Education: 95 university physics students revised test answers after chatting with an AI model, and half first got a 45-minute lesson on prompting it, spotting its reasoning errors and hallucinations, and reflecting on their own thinking. Among trained students, 69.3% of revisions turned a wrong answer right, against 41.3% for the untrained group, and 40% of untrained revisions flipped right answers to wrong by trusting the AI blindly. Trained students asked for insight into a problem twice as often and pushed back when the model misread a figure. The authors caution it was a one-day test at one university. The takeaway for today: treat the chatbot as a partner to question, not an answer machine.

Never-shown Elemore Morgan Jr. photos open at the Hilliard Friday

A new Hilliard Art Museum exhibition of 23 photographs by Louisiana photographer Elemore Morgan Jr. opens October 9 and runs through December 19, The Advocate reports.
