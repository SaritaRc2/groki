Apple launches a study app to learn how people use AI for health questions

Apple is partnering with healthcare AI company OpenEvidence on a new study app to understand how people use AI for health, TechCrunch reported on Sunday, November 15, 2026. The study is open to U.S. users over 21 with an iPhone 15 or later running iOS 26 or later with Apple Intelligence enabled, and Apple aims to enroll up to 200,000 participants for more than 12 months. Participants can ask health questions and get simplified answers grounded in clinical sources including The New England Journal of Medicine, the Journal of the American Medical Association and Nature, rate the answers and take monthly surveys. They can share HealthKit data such as resting heart rate, sleep or lab results for more personal suggestions, and the app can help prepare for a doctor’s visit. Apple said the data will be anonymized and won’t be used to train AI models, and OpenEvidence deletes query and response data after 24 hours.

OpenAI opens a science blog with a physicist’s GPT-6 Astra–assisted lattice definition of the Standard Model

OpenAI launched a separate science blog on Sunday, November 15, 2026, with a guest post by physicist Nathaniel Craig of the University of California, Santa Barbara, describing a candidate non-perturbative lattice definition of the Standard Model of particle physics built in collaboration with the GPT-6 Astra model, mlllm.io reported. An accompanying preprint is on arXiv and was reviewed by lattice specialists Maarten Golterman, Andreas Kronfeld and Yigal Shamir. According to Craig, the model proposed the method for transferring fields to finer lattices and demonstrated convergence of the comparisons. Chiral theories like the Standard Model have resisted a rigorous lattice definition for decades. If the community confirms the construction, mlllm.io notes, it would set a precedent for AI-accelerated proofs in mathematical physics.

Alibaba releases Qwen4-Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter model that cuts per-task cost by 62%

Alibaba on Monday, November 16, 2026, released Qwen4-Max, the first flagship model on its new Qwen4 architecture, mlllm.io reported. It uses a mixture-of-experts backbone with 2.4 trillion parameters, 71 billion of them active per token, accepts text, images and video, and handles contexts of up to 1 million tokens. New attention components include Gated DeltaNet, Qwen Sparse Attention and Gated Residual. On Alibaba’s internal Work360 benchmark, Qwen4-Max scored 52.69 versus 47.74 for Qwen3.8-Max, and the average cost per task fell from $4.43 to $1.67, according to the vendor’s own data. API prices are $1.50 per million input tokens and $4.50 per million output tokens. It is available via QwenCloud, Qwen Studio, QwenWork and Qoder; the weights are closed, though Alibaba promises to open smaller Qwen4 models later.

Tencent’s Hy4 flash is a budget model built to make everyday AI tasks cheap

Tencent on November 16, 2026, introduced Hy4 flash, a budget tier of its Hy4 lineup for everyday work tasks, mlllm.io reported. It is a 224-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 12 billion parameters active per token, including 64 billion parameters for an Engram conditional memory. It is natively multimodal, supports up to 1 million tokens of context and generates text at over 150 tokens per second. API rates are $0.070 per million input tokens and $0.281 per million output tokens, versus $0.834 and $2.501 for the flagship Hy4. In Tencent’s own blind test of 85 WorkBuddy users across 259 tasks, satisfaction was comparable to Claude Haiku 5.5; mlllm.io notes these are vendor figures. Tencent says the model lags in complex programming and long agent tasks. It is free to try in WorkBuddy until November 30.

Edge0 opens 145B and 120B models meant to run on consumer Nvidia GPUs, with license limits

Edge0 has published 145-billion- and 120-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model variants, extending its local-inference effort beyond earlier 35B and 8B releases, Open Model Weights reported on Sunday, November 15, 2026. The models are derivatives of Qwen3.8-Flash-Next rather than independently trained foundation models. The 145B card lists 10.12 billion active parameters per token, a 256K context configuration and a 40.81 GiB weight package, and Edge0 reports 121.2 agent-decode tokens per second on an RTX 5090 D 24 GB; Open Model Weights notes these are publisher measurements, not its own reproductions. A 24 GB GPU does not hold the larger weight set alone, so the project streams experts from system RAM. Supported builds point to Linux or Windows, an Nvidia GPU, CUDA 13 and Edge0’s own inference engine.

Innovation

MIT’s Automatar bass guitar hides an AI computer that plays harmony on its own strings

Antonis Christou, a researcher in the MIT Media Lab’s Opera of the Future group, presented Automatar, a bass guitar with an Nvidia Jetson Orin NX module embedded in its body, mlllm.io reported on Monday, November 16, 2026. The musician plays four main strings while a locally running AI model analyzes the performance and electromagnetically vibrates five additional strings, building harmony on the fly. The signal chain runs through a Bela Mini with SuperCollider, converts each string to MIDI, and the model generates sine waves that excite the extra strings through high-voltage amplifiers. The body is built from Pale Moon Ebony and mahogany, combining traditional luthier work with CNC milling and 3D printing. It is a research prototype, not a product, and a rare case of AI answering through the mechanics of strings rather than a screen.

Bharat Semi raises $24 million to put AI and vision on a single image-sensor chip

Indian semiconductor startup Bharat Semi has raised $24 million in seed funding to develop AI-enabled image sensors, TechCrunch reported on Sunday, November 15, 2026. The New Delhi-based company is building programmable sensors that combine image capture, processing and AI on one chip, which co-founder Vrinda Kapoor says could cut system costs by at least 70% compared with setups that use a separate sensor, image processor and system-on-chip. “Our aim here is to move from sensors to sense-making solutions,” Kapoor said. The pre-revenue startup reports design wins with General Atomics and India’s Kaynes Semicon and targets initial commercial production in 2027. Uses include air conditioners that detect an empty room and drones that process what they see. The round was led by Info Edge Group, South Park Commons and Indus Bridge Ventures.

A New Jersey teen taught himself machine learning to build Kora, a free app helping families read autistic children’s cues

New Jersey high schooler Shashank Madala watched adults who loved his autistic younger cousin guess, often wrongly, at why he was upset at family gatherings, Good News Network reported. At 15, with a single computer science class behind him, Madala taught himself machine learning and built Kora, a free app that offers caregivers several possible readings of what an autistic child may be feeling, with no scores, diagnoses or judgments. Early versions gave one fast, confident answer; he rebuilt it to slow adults down and to flag when it is unsure, leaving the final judgment to the adult who knows the child. Kora now has more than 700 users in 12 countries and won the Congressional App Challenge for New Jersey’s 10th District. “If you are looking for something that matters, it is probably already in your house,” he said.
