AI NewsLead

OpenAI and Anthropic say their AI refuted the Exponential Time Hypothesis, a pillar of computer science

OpenAI released solutions to three long-standing problems on Tuesday, including a surprise claimed refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH), the 1999 assumption that the fastest algorithms for certain hard logic puzzles still need exponentially more time as the puzzles grow. Hours later, Anthropic released its own refutation, TechCrunch reports. The labs claim their models found algorithms that run in “sub-exponential” time. MIT’s Ryan Williams, who once called OpenAI’s proof dumps a “DDOS attack on mathematicians,” is now organizing his field’s review with UC San Diego’s Russell Impagliazzo and Ramamohan Paturi, who first published ETH. Williams says parts of the proof look “alien” and that many papers built on ETH would need revisiting if it holds. OpenAI told him it checked and is reasonably confident the result breaks no encryption systems. None of it is peer-reviewed yet, and it would not settle P versus NP.

Experts sided with their AI agent’s hidden lean 86% of the time, Anthropic study finds

Anthropic asked 50 professional analysts to run a realistic statistical study with Claude Code and quietly set each agent to favor one of two reasonable options on four key choices, decided by coin flip. Analysts went with the agent’s lean 86% of the time, versus the roughly 50% expected if it made no difference. When analysts had already stated a preference, they switched 60% of the time if the agent disagreed, versus 6% if it agreed. Afterward, 77% used the agent’s own arguments, sometimes word for word, but only 15% credited the agent, and those who followed it closely felt just as confident and as much in charge of the decision.

Google DeepMind’s Genie 4 simulates interactive worlds, now with sound and dialogue

Google DeepMind announced Genie 4, a world model that generates real-time interactive simulations and is natively multimodal, producing music, ambient sound, and spoken dialogue along with the world. It builds on the Gemini family, with Gemini Omni grounding appearance and behavior. Set an event in motion and Genie 4 simulates what happens next, which DeepMind says can help build training environments for robots and agents. It’s a research preview for trusted testers, coming next to Google AI Ultra subscribers in Project Genie and to early-access Gemini API partners. DeepMind notes details can still drift in longer sessions, and legible text and complex physics remain hard.

Models

Liquid AI’s LFM3: six tiny open models for text, vision, and audio that run on your device

Liquid AI released LFM3, a new family of open-weight models around 2 billion parameters, all branched from one LFM3-1.7B base trained on 22 trillion tokens. The first release includes six models covering text, vision, audio, and omni input, plus two decision models. Liquid says LFM3-1.7B beats its earlier 2.6-billion-parameter model with 35% fewer parameters, the text model fits in about 1 GB at 4-bit precision with a 256K-token context window, and every model can be fine-tuned on a single GPU. They’re available on Hugging Face, Liquid’s Playground, and its API.

Innovation

A surgeon removes a prostate from 35 miles away in the first U.S. hospital-to-hospital telesurgery

Dr. Vipul Patel of AdventHealth’s Global Robotics Institute in Celebration, Florida, operated a surgical robot remotely on Sunday to remove the prostate of a 66-year-old cancer patient at an AdventHealth facility in Minneola, 35 miles away, The Guardian reports. A co-investigating surgeon was on site, and the patient was recovering well. It’s the first documented telesurgery between two U.S. locations, after 10 investigational telesurgeries between Orlando and Luanda, Angola. The study runs under an FDA investigational device exemption, which is not FDA approval, and AdventHealth says one case doesn’t establish the technology’s general performance.

Google opens its robot “brain,” Gemini Robotics ER 2, to businesses

Google made Gemini Robotics Embodied Reasoning 2 available in public preview on its Gemini enterprise platform. The model acts as a robot’s high-level brain on any body, from stationary arms to full humanoids, combining spatial and video understanding with long-horizon planning and built-in safety guardrails. Google says it can talk with people, orchestrate lower-level action models and hardware as tools, carry out multi-minute tasks involving hundreds of decisions, and coordinate teams of robots.

“The only way to predict the future is to create it”: two Boise teens build AI to save farm water

Bishop Kelly High School seniors Marco Trotta, 17, and Henry Turcotte, 18, built Irrigant, an AI system that turns weather forecasts and soil-moisture data into clear advice on when a field needs water, plus Helios, an AI agronomist farmers can text questions about their own fields. Three farmers are now piloting it across more than 7,000 acres, with talks covering 30,000 more, and the pair just hired a 16-year-old intern. “We want to create a resource that gives farmers the ability to do more with less water,” Trotta said.
