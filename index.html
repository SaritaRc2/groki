AI NewsLead

AI found two critical holes in PHP, the language behind about 70% of websites, and the patches are out: update now

PHP’s maintainers shipped PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36, and 8.2.35 on October 21 to fix two critical flaws that can let an attacker run code on a web server without logging in. Security firm Calif found one using OpenAI’s GPT-5.6 Sol as part of OpenAI’s Patch the Planet program, and Wiz Research independently found both, using its Wiz Atlas agent, Gemini 4 Argon, and GPT-5.6 Sol. Wiz, which calls the bugs PHP2Shell, says PHP powers 70% of websites, including WordPress, Drupal, and Wikipedia’s MediaWiki, that every PHP version back to 5.4 is hit by at least one flaw, and that proving the flaws could be exploited took a chain of steps “at the edge of what human researchers could practically achieve” until frontier models lowered that barrier. Wiz says it confirmed working attacks against Drupal and WordPress in August before reporting to PHP. OpenAI calls it “one of the most impactful security issues in recent memory,” released a PHP version checker as a Codex skill, and committed $1 billion in subsidized access to its security tools for defenders with limited resources. PHP 8.1 and older no longer get official updates.

AI News

Anthropic opens Claude Duet, an agent that comes to you first, sends a morning brief, and asks before it acts

Anthropic opened a research preview of Claude Duet, a new app where Claude works as a proactive agent, by waitlist for U.S. Pro and Max subscribers. Built around Claude Opus 5.5, it sends a short morning brief on the most important items across your connected apps, checks those accounts every few hours, and messages you only when something needs attention. It has its own virtual machine and browser, so it keeps working when your laptop or phone is off. Before it sends a message, makes a payment, signs a document, or deletes anything, it shows you exactly what it’s about to do and waits for approval, and some things, like trading securities or moving money between accounts, are off-limits even with approval. A new classifier blocks it from sharing credit card numbers and government ID. Anthropic says this early version may still misread a thread or misjudge what’s urgent, and it shares usage limits with the rest of Claude.

Models

Recursive releases Flint, a free coding model that runs on a laptop and that it says was largely built by AI

Recursive, the startup led by former Salesforce chief scientist Richard Socher, released its first model, Flint, on October 21, TechCrunch reports. It’s a mixture-of-experts coding model that uses 2.5 billion of its 40 billion parameters at a time, light enough for an ordinary laptop, and it’s free on Hugging Face under the open Apache 2.0 license. In Recursive’s tests it generated about 131 tokens per second on an M4 Pro MacBook Pro, roughly twice similar models, beat other models under 50 billion parameters on SWE-Bench Verified, and trailed only Alibaba’s Qwen3.8 27B on Recursive’s six-test average. Recursive says its automated research system, the Eureka Machine, helped with nearly every step, including low-bit training that shrank Flint by 71% with almost no loss in quality, letting a small team build it from scratch in under four months. The company says Flint needs more guidance than big paid coding assistants and suggests breaking large tasks into smaller steps.

Models

Inception’s Mercury 3 writes like a diffusion image model, refining whole drafts at over 1,000 tokens a second

Inception released Mercury 3 on October 19, calling it the most capable diffusion-based language model to date. Instead of writing one word at a time, a diffusion model refines a whole draft in parallel passes. Inception says Mercury 3 matches or beats GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5 across a range of benchmarks while running 4 to 8 times faster, at 1,068 tokens per second on NVIDIA GPUs, with a 1-million-token context window, up from 260,000 for Mercury 2.5. It is more than 40 times larger than Mercury 1. Regular pricing is $0.20 per million input tokens and $0.70 per million output, half off at launch, and it’s available on Inception’s platform, OpenRouter, Baseten, and Vercel. Early users include support, tutoring, and web-navigation companies; one partner, TinyFish AI, says Mercury 3 completed 50% of 50 live web-navigation tasks.

iFlytek launches Spark X2.6, a 1-million-token model it says was trained entirely on Chinese chips

At its developer festival in Beijing on October 22, iFlytek launched Spark X2.6, a mixture-of-experts model with 293 billion total and about 30 billion active parameters and a 1-million-token context window, aimed at coding and agent tasks, AGI Pulse reports. iFlytek says it was trained entirely on Chinese-made computing hardware, and chairman Liu Qingfeng claimed Spark is the only mainstream Chinese model not trained on NVIDIA GPUs. Launch pricing, 40% off, is ¥0.9 per million input tokens and ¥3.6 per million output, about $0.13 and $0.51. The flagship’s weights are closed, but a smaller sibling, Spark-X2.6-4B, is open under Apache 2.0 with the same 1-million-token context. iFlytek gave no benchmark scores for the flagship.

Innovation

Hugging Face’s Carbon-X lets researchers chat with DNA, and it runs on a laptop

Hugging Face released Carbon-X on Thursday, October 22, an open model trained on both genomic sequences and natural language so people can ask questions about how DNA changes affect gene activity and health and get conversational answers, Wired reports. It was built with researchers at Zhongguancun Academy in China and the University of Naples Federico II in Italy. The team taught an existing language model to read DNA in six-letter chunks and trained it on 300 billion tokens mixing DNA from mammals, plants, fungi, and more with English text, deliberately leaving out viruses to avoid dangerous uses. At 14 billion parameters it can run on a user’s own hardware, so sensitive patient data doesn’t have to leave the lab. It reads stretches of about 50,000 DNA letters at a time, not whole genomes. “We’ve unlocked a new capability in language models, which is fundamentally about being able to chat with DNA,” said Hugging Face’s Lewis Tunstall.

Use your head start: the PHP fix shows what happens when the good guys get there first

OpenAI says that, to its knowledge, frontier AI found the PHP flaws before attackers did, opening what it calls a “Defender’s Window” for maintainers and security providers to prepare fixes and protections before going public. Wiz reported the bugs in August, and PHP took two months to build and verify a coordinated fix. The lesson for any project: when you spot a problem early, spend the head start on preparation, not panic. And the most useful thing you can do today might be as simple as checking that your tools are up to date.
