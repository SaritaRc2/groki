Oregon and Virginia become the first states to sue SpaceX over Grok deepfake images

Virginia and Oregon have become the first states to sue Elon Musk’s SpaceX over its Grok AI image generator, CNBC reported on November 23, 2026. In separate state-court complaints, Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield and Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones argue SpaceX failed to put safeguards in place to stop Grok and X users from making and sharing harmful deepfake images, especially alleged AI-generated child sexual abuse material, and falsely represented Grok and X as generally safe in violation of state consumer-protection laws. “This content can cause fear, distress, and anxiety in those who are unwillingly exposed to the content,” Jones wrote. Both states seek injunctions, civil penalties, disgorgement and legal costs; Virginia also seeks restitution for consumers. Oregon filed in Multnomah County Circuit Court on Sunday evening and Virginia in the City of Richmond Circuit Court on Monday morning, and as of early Monday both suits were pending the courts’ acceptance. SpaceX, which already faces other Grok-related suits and probes worldwide, did not immediately respond to CNBC’s request for comment.

OpenAI’s chief scientist proposes perplexity as the yardstick for an AI speed limit

OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki has published a blog post on how to put limits on AI development, centered on a measurement called perplexity, TechCrunch reported on November 22, 2026. The debate over slowing AI has intensified since Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published a plan to “pace the frontier,” calling for “common safety standards as well as limits on the rate of unchecked AI progress.” But a speed limit needs a speedometer. Pachocki argues that benchmarks focused on AI’s “downstream capabilities” quickly go stale, while perplexity, which “measures how well a model predicts the next token on average,” has stayed “informative.” His post charts how OpenAI models’ perplexity has fallen since GPT-3.5. “Now, as we have reached losses below 0.8, we have seen the emergence of transformative capabilities in fields like cybersecurity and mathematics,” he wrote. Under his idea, companies would commit not to pass a set threshold “until our ability to understand and monitor AI behavior achieves a certain concrete milestone.” Sam Altman called the post “the most important thing OpenAI has published in a while.”

Intelligence chief warns of more tech-outage ‘bumps in the road’ as systems upgrade for the AI age

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton warned Americans to prepare for “bumps in the road” after damaging technology outages hit air travel and systems like Microsoft Outlook, CNBC reported on November 22, 2026. Officials have largely dismissed the idea that the outages, which also slowed bank transactions, stem from a coordinated or AI cyberattack. But Clayton told CNN’s “State of the Union” that disruptions may continue as companies and the government upgrade their systems. The warning comes ahead of one of the year’s busiest travel weeks. The FAA said router upgrades caused the data flood behind a nationwide ground stop just over a week ago, and Microsoft blamed its Outlook outage on “an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade.” Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CBS that bank patches may cause glitches: “I think the American people should think of it as a vaccine; your arm may hurt, you may feel a little bad the next day, but it increases your long-term immunity and resiliency.”

Walmart opens its site to Meta’s Muse agent for Black Friday shopping

Walmart will let Meta’s Muse AI agent find and buy Black Friday deals on its website on shoppers’ behalf, The Verge reported on November 23, 2026. The retailer is rolling out a live version of the Agent Gateway it announced last month, which helps AI agents find relevant products without sharing your purchase history. Walmart told The Verge after publication that the launch slipped by 24 hours, so shopping via Muse will now be available on Tuesday, November 24. Purchases run through Walmart “Agent Profiles,” a dedicated space for agent activity inside a customer’s account where you can set spending limits, approve purchases and check everything Muse has bought. While other online retailers like Amazon and eBay have blocked AI agents from browsing their platforms, Walmart says Muse support is “just the beginning,” with experiences for other agents and apps “on the way.”

Baseten releases bison-1 and groundhog-1, open-weight models that make decisions instead of chatting

Baseten has released two open-weight models built to make structured choices rather than write chat answers, Open Model Weights reported on November 22, 2026. Announced November 20, bison-1 and groundhog-1 join the Base Labs decision-model family; both accept text and images, are offered through Baseten Model APIs, and have official Hugging Face repositories exposing the weights. Baseten describes bison-1 as a dense 31-billion-parameter model built on Google Gemma 4 31B, while groundhog-1 uses the Gemma 4 26B-A4B mixture-of-experts architecture with about four billion active parameters per token. Both offer a 128K context window. Decision models evaluate explicitly defined answer choices and assign probabilities, which suits routing, prioritization and quality checks. Baseten says bison-1 scored 76 on the Artificial Analysis Decision Index v2.1, ahead of 18 other evaluated decision models, with a Brier calibration score of 0.14. The checkpoints list a combination of MIT and Apache 2.0 terms. Open Model Weights says it has not reproduced the benchmark figures.

NeuML’s Atto shrinks search models down to as few as 840 parameters

NeuML has introduced Atto, a collection of unusually compact models for embedding, ranking and related search workloads, Open Model Weights reported on November 22, 2026. In its November 19 announcement, the publisher describes a research program spanning an experimental ColBERT configuration with just 840 parameters and dense embedding checkpoints reaching 28 million parameters. The family is not one uniform model: members use different encoding approaches, sizes and embedding dimensions, with some configurations reaching 8,192 dimensions. For teams building retrieval-augmented generation or local document search, smaller encoders may cut checkpoint storage and the computation needed to produce vectors, though very wide outputs can still burden vector indexes and nearest-neighbor search, so parameter count alone is not a complete deployment metric. The model card for the inspected 28M dense variant declares Apache-2.0, but Open Model Weights says each checkpoint’s license should be verified individually. It has not reproduced NeuML’s retrieval results and calls Atto “an engineering option rather than a universal replacement for established embedding systems.”

Metric AI releases Ayb 0.5, open-weight language models built for Armenian

Metric AI Lab has introduced Ayb 0.5, a family of Armenian-focused open-weight language models, Open Model Weights reported on November 22, 2026. The November 20 release includes Ayb-0.5-0.8B, Ayb-0.5-4B and Ayb-0.5-9B, sized for budgets from lightweight text processing to larger conversational and vision-language apps. Ayb is built on Qwen3.5 rather than trained from scratch: the developers expanded the tokenizer with Armenian vocabulary, continued pretraining on Armenian and English data, and added instruction tuning and post-training. Metric AI says the 9B version supports multi-turn conversations, complex prompts and tool calling, and it kept Qwen3.5’s vision encoder so the models can answer image-based questions in Armenian. The team reports Ayb-9B scored 0.6164 on ArmBench, ranking third in its cited evaluation, with the 4B and 0.8B at 0.5828 and 0.5300. Training emphasized Eastern Armenian, so Western Armenian and dialects are less reliable. The 9B model card lists a noncommercial CC BY-NC 4.0 license, and Open Model Weights has not independently verified the benchmarks.

Quantum computing’s first real-world payoff may come in energy, from steadier grids to better solar cells

Quantum software is already being tested on the energy transition, from keeping power grids stable to finding better materials for solar cells and hydrogen production, CNBC reported on November 22, 2026, even as bigger promises like drug discovery remain years away. Phasecraft, founded in Bristol, England, in 2019, has partnered with Britain’s National Energy System Operator on grid tasks such as managing power exchanges and isolating network sections during emergencies, and says its software can complete them up to 1,000 times faster than conventional computing. It is also working with Oxford PV on more efficient photovoltaic materials and, with Johnson Matthey, Harvard and QuEra under ARPA-E oversight, on catalysts that reduce reliance on scarce metals like iridium. Quantinuum is modeling materials for hydrogen fuel cells, and Oxford spinout ORCA Computing has worked with BP on how biofuels burn. “When you get this right, the commercial returns are real: more efficient energy networks mean lower operating costs and more value from existing infrastructure,” said Phasecraft CEO Ashley Montanaro.

Claude-tuned Stockfish pushes a 1995-era 200 MHz Pentium Pro to nearly 3,200 Elo

Anthropic engineer Nicholas Carlini used Claude to adapt chess-engine code to a 200 MHz Pentium Pro with 64 MB of RAM, pushing it to nearly 3,200 Elo at 30 seconds per move, Chip Dispatch reported on November 23, 2026, citing Tom’s Hardware. He first had Claude add modern features to Crafty, a 1996 engine that scored 2,000 Elo at one second per move on the old machine; the modified version reached 3,000 at 10 seconds. He then had Claude modify Stockfish to use 32-bit arithmetic instead of 64-bit and fit its workspace inside the 64 MB of memory. After several optimization passes, Stockfish reached 2,850 Elo at one second, 3,100 at 10 seconds and almost 3,200 at 30 seconds, about 400 points above Garry Kasparov’s peak near 2,800, which under the Elo comparison means nine wins in ten. These are rating estimates, not results from a real match, Chip Dispatch noted. The point: software efficiency rather than clock speed made the difference. Carlini made the bot playable through a link on his blog post.

Look east tonight: the Beaver Moon kicks off three supermoons in a row

The next three full moons will all be supermoons, and the first, the Beaver Moon, is set to dazzle tonight and tomorrow, Good News Network reported on November 23, 2026. Its brightest moment comes at 9:40 a.m. Eastern Time on Tuesday, but nights on either side will look almost as full. Supermoons don’t look much bigger high in the sky; it’s near the horizon, framed by trees and houses, that an optical illusion makes them look huge, so the article suggests dressing warmly and stepping into an open area in early twilight to catch moonrise in the eastern sky. The name comes from the time when beavers head to their dens; the Dakota and Lakota call it the Deer Rutting Moon, the Cree the Frost Moon, and the Tlingit the Digging, or Scratching, Moon. A supermoon happens when the full Moon reaches perigee, its closest point to Earth. December’s Cold Moon will be the biggest of the three, and January’s Wolf Moon follows, all “perfect reasons to brave the cold and get out in nature.”
