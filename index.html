Nvidia plunges 14% as an SEC accounting report caps its longest weekly losing streak since 2008

Nvidia shares tumbled 14% on Friday, sliding for a seventh straight day and closing lower for a seventh consecutive week, the longest such stretch since 2008, CNBC reported on November 20, 2026. Even a better-than-expected earnings report didn’t stem the selling, though interim CEO Colette Kress projected 75% revenue growth next fiscal year and signed off Tuesday’s call by saying, “we’ve got this.”

California’s attorney general subpoenas Nvidia over Huang’s alleged China ties

California Attorney General Rob Bonta said Thursday evening that the state has served an investigative subpoena on Nvidia following news of former CEO Jensen Huang’s affair with a woman the U.S. government believes is a Chinese spy, TechCrunch reported on November 20, 2026. The state wants to find out whether Nvidia’s advanced GPUs or confidential information may have been shared with China, and whether the company misled investors and the public. “The conduct of Nvidia and its former CEO raises bright red flags, including about the ways in which the company may have misled investors,” Bonta said. “Nvidia is in a messy, transnational situation but the underlying principle of our corporate securities law is clear: Companies cannot mislead their investors,” he wrote. President Donald Trump said Friday that U.S. companies will be banned from building data centers in other countries and will face a “tremendous tax” penalty if they do, CNBC reported on November 20, 2026. “We’re going to fight very hard for the data centers,” Trump said at a White House press conference, in a policy announcement that apparently came as news to his own staff. “I don’t know if that’s a breaking story,” he said. “I haven’t even said that to my people. Signal blocks screenshots so AI agents like Gemini and Siri can’t read your chats

Signal is restricting screenshots on Android and iOS in an effort to keep AI agents from reading users’ messages, The Verge reported on November 20, 2026. Agentic AI built into operating systems that analyzes on-screen information is “undermining Signal’s ability to independently guarantee privacy,” Signal president Meredith Whittaker said in a blog post. Blocking screenshots in the app on iOS 27 and Android should prevent Siri, Gemini and other Android assistants from reading on-screen content; users can turn screenshots back on in settings, but Whittaker warns that doing so will “potentially expose your (and your contacts’) Signal content to these AI agents, and break our privacy guarantees.” Signal took a similar approach last year against Microsoft’s Recall feature, and is now calling on Apple, Google and Microsoft to provide “simple technical tools and accurate documentation” to developers. “Signal is the canary in the coal mine, but this affects the entire ecosystem and should galvanize anyone who cares about these fundamental rights,” Whittaker said.

Cursor tells lawmakers it may phase out Chinese AI models over ‘geopolitical risk’

SpaceX’s AI coding platform Cursor is considering moving away from Chinese AI models because of “geopolitical risk,” CNBC reported on November 20, 2026. Cursor, which SpaceX acquired at an implied $60 billion equity value in August, built its coding model Composer 2 on top of Moonshot AI’s Kimi K2.5. It now plans to “explore phasing out PRC-developed open-weight models because of geopolitical risk,” according to a report by the House Committee on Homeland Security and the House Select Committee on China, shared with CNBC ahead of its Friday publication. Airbnb and DoorDash also engaged with the months-long investigation. The report found that cost, customization and near-frontier performance were driving adoption of Chinese models, with the U.S. lacking a competitive open-weight alternative at the “price and performance the market demands.” Its recommendations include a “national American open-weight initiative” combining public computing power, shared evaluation infrastructure and “lawful access” to American frontier models for training.

Tencent publishes open weights for its full 770-billion-parameter Hy4 flagship

Tencent has published downloadable weights for its full flagship Hy4 model, Open Model Weights reported on November 21, 2026, completing a release it announced on November 4 with weights promised to follow. The official repository now lists three artifacts: instruction-tuned Hy4, an FP8 instruction checkpoint and a pretrained Hy4 base. Tencent describes Hy4 as a multimodal mixture-of-experts model with a 770-billion-parameter language backbone and 49 billion active parameters per token, plus a 1.23-billion-parameter vision transformer and a 3.3-billion-parameter speculative-decoding drafter. It understands text, images and video and advertises a context ceiling of 1,048,576 tokens, with deployment recipes for vLLM and SGLang. The weights ship under Tencent’s own Hy License, not Apache 2.0, Open Model Weights cautioned. Tencent reports strong results on computer use, office workflows and research tasks, while acknowledging gaps against top proprietary systems; Open Model Weights says it has not reproduced those claims.

Thinking Machines’ Inkling 2 Mini loops its layers to run coding agents on a laptop

Thinking Machines Lab has released Inkling 2 Mini, an open-weight model built to make coding-agent work feasible on local computers, Open Model Weights reported on November 21, 2026. Published November 20, it is a 26.4-billion-parameter mixture of experts with about 3.4 billion parameters active, shipped in a mixed NVFP4 and MXFP8 checkpoint of roughly 20GB; the company recommends 32GB or more of memory. Its novelty is a loop: of 24 physical decoder layers, the twelve middle ones run three times with shared parameters, producing 48 layer applications per token. It accepts text and images, has a published context ceiling of one million tokens, and has day-one recipes for Transformers, llama.cpp, MLX on Apple silicon and SGLang. Thinking Machines reports strong coding-agent, search and tool-use results against similarly sized open models and discloses weaker results on some instruction-following tests. The weights are Apache 2.0 with a separate acceptable-use policy.

Microsoft releases Aion, a small MIT-licensed model meant to power future Windows features

Microsoft’s Windows Applied Sciences Group has published Aion 1.0 Instruct, a 2.3-billion-parameter language model for local text tasks, Open Model Weights reported on November 19, 2026. The Hugging Face model card declares an MIT license and lists weights, configuration, tokenizer and code. The model has a 32,768-token context limit and a hybrid attention stack of 21 sliding-window layers and seven full-attention layers, and supports eight languages: English, German, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, French, Chinese and Japanese. It targets everyday work such as summarizing and rewriting documents, answering questions and understanding email threads. Microsoft says an optimized version, paired with a content-moderation model, is planned to become a Windows small language model by the end of January 2027; that integration has not happened yet. Microsoft self-reports 55.15 on MMLU and 83.90 on its IFEval aggregation. Open Model Weights notes the quickstart requires trust_remote_code, which deserves careful review.

Super-hot rock geothermal heats up as Google signs a $500 million-plus deal with Quaise

Geothermal startups are racing to tap the deepest, hottest rocks, and the winners could power the grid and AI data centers, TechCrunch reported on November 20, 2026. At extreme depths water turns into a supercritical fluid that carries tremendous energy, and a single super-hot rock well could generate 15 megawatts or more. Quaise Energy said Tuesday that Google will pay more than $500 million to secure 50 megawatts from a Quaise project in central Oregon that will tap heat three miles deep. Starting about 2.5 miles down, Quaise swaps its drill bit for one powered by a gyrotron that bombards rock with millimeter-wave radiation, turning it to ash. Rival Mazama Energy announced Carbon Chill, which uses carbon dioxide instead of drilling mud to keep conventional bits cool. Mazama drilled its second well 80% faster than its first, and the CO2 cooling cut drilling time another 20%, CEO Sriram Vasantharajan told TechCrunch. The U.S. has up to 57 terawatts of geothermal potential.

Daniel Ek backs Europa Fusion, an AI researcher’s simulation-first fusion startup

Spotify founder Daniel Ek is backing Europa Fusion, a stealthy U.K. fusion power startup founded by AI researcher Nantas Nardelli, TechCrunch reported in an exclusive on November 20, 2026, after Bloomberg first reported the investment. Founded in February, the startup is led by Nardelli, who previously worked at Google DeepMind, Facebook AI Research and Microsoft Research Cambridge and was recently head of machine learning at Pacific Fusion. Where most fusion startups lean heavily on physical test devices, Nardelli believes fusion companies should invest in a more simulation- and data-heavy approach, a source told TechCrunch. Like Pacific Fusion, Europa will pursue pulsed power, using massive, perfectly timed surges of electrical current to compress fuel targets until atoms fuse. No one has yet built a fusion plant that supplies electricity to the grid, but the technology could ease worries about AI’s energy use. “I used to build robots for a living,” Nardelli wrote. “One day I’ll be back to that, but it’s time to fix climate change for good.”

A father-daughter business turns rhino dung into paper and gives villagers a reason to protect rhinos

A father-daughter team in Assam, India, makes paper from rhino dung collected by locals, giving villagers who sometimes clash with rhinos a steady income and a reason to protect them, Good News Network reported on November 20, 2026. Elrhino Stationery co-founder Mahesh Chandra Bora, a retired mining engineer, got the idea after reading about rhino dung paper in Rajasthan and wondered why it couldn’t be done in Assam, home to some 2,900 greater one-horned rhinos. His first tests used his wife’s kitchen blender. His daughter Nisha left a 17-year corporate career to help, and Elrhino launched in 2014. Rhino and elephant dung harvested near reserves like Kaziranga and Pobitora national parks is washed and boiled to recover plant fibers, pulped, mixed with recycled paper or cotton waste, then pressed and dried into sheets. The range now includes notebooks, journals, paper bags, lampshades and playing cards.
