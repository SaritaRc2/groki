AI NewsLead

Anthropic gives METR researchers employee-like access to audit its models from the inside

Anthropic announced on Thursday, October 15, that it has onboarded six employees from METR, a nonprofit that evaluates frontier AI models, with company hardware, office space, and access to internal tools “roughly on par” with what its own risk-assessment teams see. Outside narrow categories such as customer data, attorney-client privilege, and model-architecture details, staff are encouraged to share information with them as with a fellow alignment colleague. METR will publish its own analysis of Anthropic’s risk levels, incidents, and practices; Anthropic cannot veto publication but may require redactions in those predefined categories, and METR may say publicly whether it believes the redactions were justified. The arrangement sits at the top of a three-tier evaluator system; SecureBio and Accenture (with Faculty) are at earlier, more limited tiers, and Anthropic says it is in early talks with Hugging Face and others. The company says it is not funding METR’s work, and that the step follows its White House Accord pledge and CEO Dario Amodei’s call to embed independent evaluators.

Simons Foundation, OpenAI, and Anthropic put $15 million behind helping mathematicians navigate AI proofs

The Simons Foundation announced on October 15 a partnership with Anthropic and OpenAI committing $15 million in combined funding and resources so mathematicians can evaluate AI-assisted results, learn new tools, share perspectives, and shape collective standards for AI use. The foundation will manage and award the money through an open call for proposals supporting workshops and related programs. OpenAI separately said it is contributing $5 million to the initiative and will provide model and compute access to supported researchers; Anthropic and OpenAI will also supply tools and compute that can extend beyond mathematics into basic science. Foundation president David Spergel said the community—not any single company—should drive how the tools are evaluated and adopted. The Independent and other outlets noted the gift follows backlash after OpenAI published a large batch of experimental AI-generated proofs that mathematicians said overwhelmed traditional review.

NYC Comptroller Mark Levine urges Anthropic to pause its IPO until AI safety and governance catch up

New York City Comptroller Mark Levine wrote Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei on October 14 urging the company to pause its planned IPO until it fully and transparently addresses AI safety, governance, and development-pace concerns, a letter his office published with October 15 coverage. Levine oversees public pension funds with more than $325 billion in assets. He cites Amodei’s September essay calling to slow capability gains, draft S-1 risk language that reportedly warns of catastrophic or existential risks, and governance arrangements that may concentrate control with founders and trustees. Before going public, he asks Anthropic to disclose specific audits and controls, their scope, a timeline, and how those steps would support slowing capability development. Bloomberg Law and Reuters reported the call; Levine requested a written response.

NVIDIA opens Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, a 120B multimodal agent model, plus Bolt edge variants

NVIDIA on October 15 released Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, an open 120-billion-parameter multimodal mixture-of-experts model aimed at always-on enterprise agents that need instruction following, tool use, multistep workflows, and visual understanding of pages, PDFs, charts, and tables. The company positions it as about one-fifth the size of its largest reasoning model while matching that class on specialized agentic tasks, and as up to 5× faster than the next-best open peer in its Artificial Analysis class comparison. Organizations can post-train it on their own data; early customizers named in NVIDIA’s post include Scale AI, AfterQuery, Mercor, CrowdStrike, Salesforce, Perplexity, and others. Two edge companions arrive as Nemotron 3.5 Bolt: a 74B-A9B 24GB variant available now for local RTX/DGX Spark agent work, and a 4GB E4B conversational edge model due October 23. Super VL weights are on Hugging Face, OpenRouter, ModelScope, and as NVIDIA NIM.

OpenAI’s Rosalind Academic Plan gives U.S. university labs free seats and GPT-Rosalind access

OpenAI on October 15 introduced the Rosalind Academic Plan for eligible U.S. university labs, offering up to 30 ChatGPT Business Standard seats at no cost for up to 12 months plus access to GPT-Rosalind—its life-sciences model and Rosalind workbench for chemistry, genomics, wet-lab assistance, and related scientific tool use—subject to trusted-access approval. Research data shared under the plan is not used to train OpenAI models by default; API access and credits are not included. PIs and lab leads without an existing Enterprise agreement apply through trusted access; labs at universities with Enterprise must go through their institutional process. OpenAI cites early academic users including labs at UCSF, Stanford/Arc Institute, and others, and says it verifies institutional legitimacy, PI identity, research intent, and a designated safety contact before activation.

Innovation

LLMs that evolve specialist heuristics beat prior AI methods on 36 combinatorial optimization benchmarks

Researchers from Nanyang Technological University and collaborators report in Nature Machine Intelligence (published October 1, 2026) a framework called LACE—LLM-driven Algorithm Construction via Complementary Evolution—that splits algorithm design into a verified input/output/tool/heuristic interface and then evolves a portfolio of specialist heuristics under time budgets. Across 36 classical CO-Bench problems, LACE averaged 0.945 versus 0.870 for the strongest prior LLM-based method and 0.571 for direct prompting with no framework. On four structurally new problems, LACE scored 0.97–0.99 while five existing LLM baselines failed to produce any feasible algorithm. Code and data are open on GitHub and Zenodo. The work targets manufacturing, logistics, healthcare operations, and energy scheduling where hand-designed heuristics usually take months of expert iteration.

October 1, 2026Nature Machine Intelligence

Innovation

Google’s open MedGemma medical vision-language models land in Nature Medicine

A Google Research and DeepMind team reports in Nature Medicine (published October 6, 2026) MedGemma, a family of medical vision-language foundation models built on Gemma 3—including multimodal 4B and 27B variants plus a 27B text model—and MedSigLIP, a medically tuned vision encoder. On out-of-distribution tasks the authors report gains of 2.6–10% in medical image question answering, 15.5–18.1% in chest X-ray finding classification, and 10.8% in agentic evaluations versus the base models, while keeping general Gemma capabilities largely intact. Fine-tuning MedGemma was more data-efficient than fine-tuning base Gemma 3 on several medical tasks. Models and example code are released via Google’s Health AI Developer Foundations; the authors stress clinical validation remains essential before real-world use.

Motivation

Moroccan teens say AI helps them learn—and warn about the ‘laziness paradox’

A mixed-methods study in Discover Education (published October 6, 2026) surveyed 150 secondary students in Morocco’s Casablanca-Settat region and interviewed 12 of them about AI-assisted learning. Students reported positive perceptions across learning outcomes, motivation, self-regulated learning, and digital literacy, with a strong link between perceived outcomes and motivation. ChatGPT was the most-used tool, usually on personal phones as a homework aid. Qualitative themes included clearer explanations and less stress, conditional trust via cross-checking course materials, worry about AI mistakes in STEM, and a “laziness paradox” when ready-made answers reduce cognitive effort. The authors urge schools to teach responsible use—critique and verify, don’t ban blindly—and to redesign homework so students evaluate AI output instead of submitting it raw.

Motivation

Read beside yesterday’s UMD trial: AI can light the spark, but you still have to do the reps

Yesterday’s sheet carried a University of Maryland–led randomized trial of 2,379 undergraduates in which course-integrated generative AI tutor access was associated with lower final grades and less LMS participation. Set that next to today’s Moroccan classroom study: teens feel more motivated and organized with AI, yet name the laziness trap themselves. The GROKI takeaway for Friday stays practical. Use the model to clarify a stuck step, then close the chat and finish the problem in your own handwriting. Check answers against the textbook or a teacher before you trust a fluent reply. Motivation that outsources the struggle tends to fade; motivation that uses AI as a coach tends to stick.
