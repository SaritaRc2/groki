Mistral releases the weights of Le Chonk, its 1-trillion-parameter Mistral Large 4

Mistral released open weights for an improved Mistral Large 4, nicknamed Le Chonk, a natively multimodal model with 1 trillion total parameters that entered public preview on October 6. Mistral says three more weeks of reinforcement learning since the preview brought big gains in coding and defensive cybersecurity, that it leads open-weight models in both, and that it matches the best on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index while using fewer tokens. It has three reasoning modes and is trained in Europe. Anyone can download it from Hugging Face and run it with vLLM or SGLang under a modified MIT license that is free to use, modify, and commercialize; only companies selling it as a hosted model service need a separate license. API pricing is $0.70 per million input tokens and $2.10 per million output tokens.

OpenAI rewrites its Model Spec around five values, starting with faithfulness to human intent

OpenAI updated the Model Spec, its public description of how its models should behave, reorganizing it around five values: faithfulness to human intent, harm minimization, honesty and transparency, human agency, and care. OpenAI says models taking more consequential actions need to understand what people intend and value, not just follow literal instructions, while staying under human oversight. The revision adds guidance on context, oversight, and agency, adjusts some behavioral defaults, and retires much of the detailed style guidance.

Innovation

GitHub’s Copilot Fusion picks the right AI model for each step, as agents now start 40% of pull requests

At GitHub Universe, GitHub launched Copilot Fusion, which decides which model, or mix of models, should handle each step of a coding task. Microsoft’s Jay Parikh wrote that Fusion Max scored within two points of Claude Opus 5.5 Max on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index at half the cost and 40% less time per task. GitHub’s Octoverse report, out the same day, says more than 40% of pull requests started from an agent’s draft by September, up from 17% in March, and developers pushed code 17.9 billion times in the year to September 30.

Pika’s open-weight H3 Flex makes 15-second video with sound in seconds

Pika introduced H3 Flex, its post-trained version of MiniMax H3, built for creative professionals who iterate. Pika says it can generate a 15-second 768p clip with synchronized audio in as little as seven seconds of compute, by cutting generation from 50 steps to six. It takes up to 12 reference images, exports five separate audio stems, and finishes at up to 4K. Pika is releasing open weights and inference code, and says Flex ranks No. 1 among open-weight models on DesignArena’s text-to-video leaderboard.

Canva unveils Brush, a model that sees your design and edits it on request

Canva introduced Brush, a vision-language model for design from its 160-person Canva Research lab. Brush looks at how a design is put together and makes the changes you ask for, from one line of copy to every slide in a deck. Canva says it is up to 69% faster for general editing and 46% cheaper per edit than its previous design agent. It arrives a week after Canva’s first image model, Craft, and is rolling out first to Canva Pro subscribers.

Innovation

Clover writes code in blocks instead of one word at a time

DiffuSpace released open weights for Clover-35B-A3B-Preview, a diffusion language model for coding and agents. Instead of producing one token after another, it writes in blocks and refines a noisy draft of each whole block at once. The team reports 73.6 on SWE-bench Verified and 515 tokens per second on agent tasks on a single H200 GPU. It’s a mixture-of-experts model with about 3 billion of its 35 billion parameters active per token. No independent evaluation has been published yet.

Innovation

RoboJEPA shows robot ‘imagination’ improves predictably with more compute

Researchers from Meta and Mila presented RoboJEPA, an 8-billion-parameter world model trained on real data from 12 kinds of robots. They found its prediction error follows a power law in compute, so quality can be forecast well beyond the scale they tested, and that real-robot planning improves predictably too. Planning toward a single goal image, it handled long-horizon tasks on real hardware without extra training. The team calls it the first scaling law for multi-robot world models trained on real data and has released all checkpoints and code.

Motivation

An Ohio 17-year-old built an AI app to get more people screened for lung cancer

Syna Bhattacharya, a 17-year-old senior in Chagrin Falls, Ohio, built PulmoSmart AI after learning that more than 80% of people eligible for lung cancer screening never get screened. Users enter their age and smoking history to see if they qualify, and the app’s navigation AI points them to the nearest screening site. With 14 student ambassadors she has reached more than 500 people, and she is one of 20 students nationwide named Phase 2 finalists with the American Lung Cancer Screening Initiative.

UTSA students used data and AI to help San Antonio veterans find housing

UT San Antonio Rowdy Corps scholars teamed up with the American GI Forum’s My City Is My Home to study more than 1,200 apartment records and 1,600 single-family rental listings and map the barriers residents face. They strengthened the group’s Housing Finder database and tested and refined Housing Helper, a free AI tool that guides renters, homeowners, veterans, seniors, voucher holders, and families to next steps and local resources.
