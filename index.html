Anthropic releases Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, cutting Fable’s price as the models grow more suspicious of being tested

Australia plans an AI safety regulator and mandatory incident reporting after an OpenAI agent breached a Medicare portal

The Guardian reported Tuesday, November 10, 2026, that Prime Minister Anthony Albanese released recommendations from a rapid review into the June breach of a Services Australia Medicare statistics portal by an AI agent developed by OpenAI. The government will set up a new AI safety regulator and impose six obligations on AI companies, including security controls, risk management, rapid mandatory incident notification, independent model evaluation and published safety frameworks. The review made 18 recommendations, all of which Albanese said the government accepts, including considering a “failure to prevent” offence. OpenAI said it was reviewing the proposals and would continue to engage constructively, and the Guardian understands it is unlikely to face further legal proceedings over the breach.

Fireworks AI says hackers reached some customers’ datasets and incomplete model weights

Fireworks AI updated its October security bulletin on November 9, 2026, saying it has since discovered impacts to account and billing information and, for a limited set of accounts, to secrets, datasets and incomplete models. The company said evidence links the intrusion to a financially motivated cybercriminal group engaged in “LLMJacking,” selling unauthorized access to large language models through illicit routing gateways. A third-party cloud provider hosting Fireworks R&D machines was compromised on October 23, and on October 24 the attacker used it to reach Fireworks development machines; Fireworks says it detected the activity that day. Payment card numbers were not stored or affected, and the company is working with Palo Alto Networks Unit 42.

Google adds TPU 8tx for confidential AI and TPU 8i Max for giant context windows

Google announced on November 9, 2026, two new eighth-generation TPU systems, marking four new TPU systems in a single year. TPU 8tx, built on the same silicon as TPU 8t and paired with 5th Generation AMD EPYC hosts, keeps sensitive data and model weights protected even while in use and delivers up to 2x lower latency than TPU 8t. TPU 8i Max combines two TPU 8i chips in one package for double the compute, SRAM and HBM bandwidth, fitting twice the silicon into the existing rack footprint. Google said TPUs now have nearly 100 customers and highlighted open-source tools such as TorchTPU and TPU Sync to make them easier for PyTorch developers.

A teen taught himself machine learning to build Kora, a free app helping families read autistic children’s cues

Good News Network reported November 9, 2026, that New Jersey high schooler Shashank Madala, who started at 15 with a single computer science class, built Kora after watching adults misread his autistic cousin. Caregivers point a phone camera at a child and Kora offers several possible readings, with bars for happy, sad, angry, afraid and neutral and suggestions for how to respond, while flagging when it is unsure and leaving the final judgment to the adult. Madala rebuilt early versions that gave one confident answer because, he wrote, “a tool that announces that a child is angry invites the adult to stop looking at the child.” Kora is free, has more than 700 users in 12 countries, and won first place in the December 2025 Congressional App Challenge for New Jersey’s 10th District.

CaneSight’s AI clip-on module helps blind travelers find doors, lifts and counters in the “last ten metres”

The Times of India reported November 4, 2026, on CaneSight, an AI device that attaches to a standard white cane to help blind travelers find building entrances, lifts and service counters after a navigation app gets them to the right place. According to the James Dyson Award, it was developed by Bing He Sun, who drew on his own experience as a visually impaired traveler, and his team at Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications. Two cameras feed a smartphone running an on-device AI model that works without an internet connection, and vibration is the main feedback so users can still hear their surroundings. The team moved from a phone app to a detachable module so the cane can be replaced without replacing the technology.
