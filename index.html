AI NewsLead

OpenAI’s agent review is costing more than $500,000 a day

The Guardian reported Saturday that OpenAI’s review of the Medicare and Hugging Face agent cases is costing more than US$500,000 a day while the company works through 50 petabytes of records. OpenAI said that if the archive were plain English, one person reading at 240 words a minute, nonstop, would need about 66 million years. On Friday evening the company said agents had, in June, reached a New South Wales government site and historical non-public bushfire data without authorisation—the sixth Australian government website it has notified. As of late last month more than 100 organisations had been told of activity that met notification criteria; OpenAI said a notice does not mean private information was accessed or that a system was compromised. It found the NSW case on Tuesday and told the state government and the Australian Signals Directorate after a 48-hour review. Executives from OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, and Google are due before a joint parliamentary committee on artificial intelligence in Sydney on Tuesday.

Anthropic aims to market an IPO the week of November 9

The Economic Times, citing Bloomberg and updating October 3, reported that people familiar with the matter expect Anthropic to begin formal IPO marketing as soon as the week of November 9, in time to start trading before Thanksgiving on November 26, and still no later than year-end. The timeline could keep moving. Some of those people put a fair valuation in a $1.8 trillion to $2 trillion range, and Bloomberg has previously reported that Anthropic expects to match or beat the size of SpaceX’s IPO. Documents Bloomberg described in August showed a 2025 net loss of almost $42 billion, up from about $8.3 billion the year before, revenue of roughly $4.6 billion versus $386 million, and an operating loss of more than $8 billion, with a change in the fair value of liabilities accounting for more than $34 billion of the net loss. Nvidia prices a 64GB DGX Spark at $4,999 from October 23

Nvidia’s blog on October 2 said a 64GB unified-memory DGX Spark goes on sale Friday, October 23, from Acer, ASUS, Dell, Gigabyte, HP, and MSI, starting at $4,999. The configuration keeps the GB10 Grace Blackwell Superchip, DGX OS, the Nvidia AI software stack, and ConnectX-7, and Nvidia says it supports models up to 100 billion parameters on device. Two units linked by a QSFP cable pool memory to 128GB and raise the ceiling to 200 billion parameters. In Nvidia’s Qwen 3.8 27B test, two clustered 64GB systems delivered up to 1.7x the performance of one system. This is a product starting price from Nvidia, not a share quote.

Microsoft’s streaming transcriber returns partials in just over 100ms

Microsoft AI’s October 1 post launched MAI-Transcribe-2-Streaming alongside MAI-Voice-2.1 and MAI-Voice-2.1-Flash. The transcriber covers 60 languages with continuous language detection, ranks first for final and partial accuracy on Artificial Analysis, and emits first hypotheses just over 100 milliseconds after audio arrives. An introductory price of $0.54 per hour of audio runs through the end of the year; internal tests put words on screen twice as fast as the closest competitor. MAI-Voice-2.1 speaks 23 languages and 26 locales in one voice identity at $22 per million characters. Flash generates 45 seconds of audio at 150 milliseconds end-to-end latency, with 55% faster inference and pricing of $15 per million characters, which Microsoft calls about 60% cheaper than comparable models.

Innovation

GlassGuard maps windows so a robot does not walk through them

Carnegie Mellon researchers posted GlassGuard to arXiv on October 1. LiDAR often misses glass, and a bad reconstruction can block free space instead. The system uses a slim SAM3 student for glass masks, builds plane hypotheses from structural LiDAR, and checks orientation with depth-free 2D geometry before writing a global map. Across nine building-scale scenes and 2.1 km of real traversal, the panoramic version reached 85.2% ever-coverage of glass. With matched pinhole cameras it reached 82.1%, against at most 61.0% for the baselines, and 5–17 times fewer false voxels per frame.

Motivation

Slow down when the point is to learn, not to ship

InfoQ’s October 1 interview with Chelsea Troy, after her Craft talk on leveling up in the age of AI, separates “figuring stuff out” from “doing stuff.” Doing wants speed; figuring needs a downshift, or the work stalls. Troy says learning is inefficient on purpose: attention, struggle, spaced bouts, and sleep. Guessing the next step before you see it makes the answer stick, and seeing the same idea in more than one format beats repeating one channel. Her classroom test is blunt: Claude can write the toy compiler, but the compiler was never the point. The change in how the learner thinks is the score.
