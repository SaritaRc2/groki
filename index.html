Gemini 4 Argon opens to everyone: $2/$10 per million tokens, a 1M-token limit, and #1 on the Vals Index

Google made Gemini 4 Argon globally available on October 13—to developers and enterprises through the Gemini API in AI Studio, to Google AI Ultra subscribers in Google Antigravity, and to Ultra and Pro subscribers in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search, Google DeepMind’s Koray Kavukcuoglu wrote. Argon launches at an introductory $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens (cached input 95% off) until November 30, after which $4/$20 applies, and offers a 1M unified input-output token limit with a new continuation protocol. Google says Argon ranks #1 on the Vals Index, helped Antigravity teams lift agentic code submissions by more than 35% while cutting rollbacks by nearly a third, sped Gemma 4 E2B inference 2.85x on an A100, and scored 87% on the ERQA embodied-reasoning benchmark. On safety, Google reports shortcut attempts on coding benchmarks dropped to 2% and Gray Swan prompt-injection attack success fell to 0.7%. Google is also offering 10,000 scientists its top Antigravity tier free for six months, and cyber defenders can apply to the Fairwind Program.

FBI arrests an OpenAI engineer accused of hiding a recording device in an executive conference room

OpenAI said Tuesday the FBI has arrested one of its employees in connection with a sophisticated listening device hidden in one of its offices, NBC News reports from Reuters. The Justice Department said OpenAI staff found the audio recorder plugged into a USB port of a television in an executive conference room in a San Francisco building; it had been concealed in toilet paper, installed “on or about Sept. 28” and discovered “on or about Sept. 30.” OpenAI said it used its internal AI models to identify the person and trace the device, and that “At this time, we have no indications of this individual inappropriately accessing or exfiltrating model weights, source code, or customer data. The employee was terminated.” FBI Director Kash Patel said the engineer “allegedly concealed a covert recording device inside his employer’s executive conference room to secretly capture sensitive communications about the company’s super intelligence technology.” The complaint says a similar device was found in an unattended bag on October 5; the next court date is October 19.

Reuters: the Fed, OCC, FDIC, Bank of England, and ECB step up questions to banks about AI agents and cyber recovery

Banking regulators are stepping up efforts to contain growing AI risks, six people familiar with the matter told Reuters on October 13. Three U.S. regulators, including the Federal Reserve, are quizzing banks on whether they deploy Chinese-developed technology, use AI agents in cyber defence, and can control increasingly autonomous systems—what limits are placed on agents, whether their decisions can be audited, whether humans stay involved in key decisions, and how the systems can be shut down. In Europe, the Bank of England and the European Central Bank are pressing lenders to improve recovery plans; the ECB gave banks an end-October deadline for plans to counter AI-enabled cyber threats. Regulators are still gathering information and are not yet being prescriptive, the sources said. “The adoption of AI is often outpacing the ability to put the controls in place,” said Grant Thornton’s Michael Woodbridge. “To protect against attacks, rapid recovery matters as much as prevention,” said BCG’s Sukand Ramachandran.

Microsoft bets on its own silicon: Maia 300 arrives in 2027 as the main engine for its AI products

Microsoft said on October 12 it will scale its Azure Maia chip family to multi-gigawatt deployments starting in 2027. “We will scale our Azure Maia family to multi-gigawatt deployments starting in 2027, making Maia the primary inference system for Microsoft products and services,” Scott Guthrie, executive vice president of Cloud + AI, wrote. Maia 200 is already serving production workloads including Microsoft Copilot. Microsoft expects Maia 300 to deliver 40% more tokens per megawatt than leading GPU-based systems and up to 7x the rack-scale token throughput of Maia 200 in a 96-accelerator configuration; each chip carries eight HBM4 stacks with 18.0 TB/s of peak memory bandwidth in a 950-watt TDP, and compute chiplets are built on TSMC’s N2P process. Production deployments begin in 2027. These are company expectations, not independent results. On Tuesday Microsoft rose 0.43%, among the Dow’s risers in Sharecast’s close report.

Liquid AI’s open-weight LFM2.5-24B-A2B runs agents on your own machine with just 2B active parameters

Liquid AI released LFM2.5-24B-A2B on October 13, a 24-billion-parameter reasoning model with only 2 billion active parameters per token, built for fast, private agents on-device, the team wrote on Hugging Face. It always thinks before answering, calls tools, and works inside agent harnesses such as OpenClaw, Hermes Agent, Pi, and OpenCode. Liquid reports 184 tokens per second on an Apple M5 Max and up to 21K output tokens per second on a single NVIDIA B300; context grows to 131K tokens and pre-training doubled to about 33T tokens. On Liquid’s own benchmark table it scores 48.09 on LiveClaw versus 38.19 for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and more than triples the next model on AutomationBench, while larger models keep the edge on GPQA Diamond and SWE-Bench Verified. Both the model and its base checkpoint are open-weight on Hugging Face, with day-one support in llama.cpp, MLX, vLLM, SGLang, and ONNX.

Innovation

A Penn platform lets epilepsy patients talk with their EEG in real time, and their feedback cut false seizure alarms by about 80%

University of Pennsylvania researchers report in Nature Medicine on October 13 a device platform that converses with patients to help manage epilepsy: it links scalp and intracranial EEG to a secure, LLM-based AI that talks with patients through a smartphone app, sends alerts and surveys, answers questions about seizures and sleep, and uses patient feedback to retrain its seizure detector. Tested with 13 inpatients during video-EEG monitoring, the system exchanged 1,307 messages; its data agent answered retrieval questions with 92.1% accuracy and usability scored 83/100. In a pseudoprospective simulation, fine-tuning from patients’ own app annotations reduced false alarms by 80.9% without clinician input; adding clinician review cut them 82.6%. Every interaction was reviewed daily by humans and ran on HIPAA-compliant infrastructure. The authors stress the small, short inpatient sample and plan outpatient trials, including one for Parkinson’s deep brain stimulation.

Innovation

A robot chemist trained in a digital twin made 3,000 decisions in 30 days and found four highly selective reactions

Researchers at the Polish Academy of Sciences’ Institute of Organic Chemistry report in Nature Communications on October 13 a bespoke robotic platform guided by deep reinforcement learning that autonomously explores asymmetric reactions—scouting conditions, discovering chiral products, screening catalysts, and mapping substrate scope. Its control policies were trained first in a digital twin of asymmetric catalysis, then transferred to the lab. Validated on chiral phosphoric acid catalysis, the system made more than 3,000 decisions over 30 days and uncovered four highly enantioselective transformations, including an axially chiral molecule and three allene syntheses, one involving enantioselective functionalization of a pyrrolopyrrole moiety. The authors present deep reinforcement learning-guided autonomy as a strategy for discovery in complex catalytic systems; the paper is open access.

October 13, 2026Nature Communications

An AI learning companion that hints instead of telling cut fifth-graders’ math anxiety and lifted motivation

In a randomized experiment with 174 fifth-grade students in China, an AI-supported gamified math program lowered mathematics anxiety and raised learning motivation more than both the same games without AI and traditional lecture teaching, researchers from Universiti Sains Malaysia report in BMC Psychology (published October 3). The two-week program’s AI companion was set up not to hand over answers: it asked guiding questions first, escalated through clue, reasoning, and worked-example hints only after repeated errors, and offered encouragement such as “Don’t worry, let’s try again together!” Students could pick a scholarly, humorous, or adventurous personality. The motivation gain was concentrated in intrinsic motivation and perceived competence. Both gamified groups beat lectures on achievement, but the AI version did not significantly beat the plain games there—gamification drove the scores, while the companion helped with anxiety and drive. The authors note the proposed mechanisms were not directly tested.

Motivation

Write your own questions first: teachers who let AI design the questioning got weaker discussions—and felt more confident

A year-long video study of 90 junior secondary math teachers and 897 lessons in eastern China, published October 6 in Frontiers in Psychology, finds that what teachers hand to AI matters more than how much they use it. Delegating wording and context materials came with longer, more varied problem sequences and no detectable cost. Delegating the questioning sequence itself came with fewer higher-order questions, less uptake of student ideas, and more unplanned student contributions left unrecognized—while teachers’ confidence rose. When randomly assigned lessons required teachers to draft their own questioning and variation sequence before consulting the model, substantive uptake of student talk improved. Total time spent with AI predicted none of the lesson-level outcomes once the type of delegation was known. “What predicted classroom discourse was which layer was delegated, not how much,” the authors write. The takeaway for any learner or builder: keep the thinking-design step yours, and let the tool polish.
