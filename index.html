Jensen Huang is out at Nvidia, and CFO Colette Kress steps in as interim CEO

Jensen Huang has been ousted as CEO of Nvidia after reports that he had affairs with both an alleged Chinese spy and one of his direct reports, TechCrunch reported Thursday, November 12, 2026. Huang had been so central to the company that he never established a succession plan. While Nvidia conducts a more thorough search for a successor, CFO and Executive Vice President Colette Kress has taken over as interim CEO of a company worth about $3.8 trillion. Kress, 59, has worked at Nvidia since September 2013, after three years as a CFO at Cisco and 13 years at Microsoft. “I am committed to building on the extraordinary foundation Jensen created over the last 33 years,” Kress said in a statement, adding that the company’s focus remains on customers, partners, developers and shareholders. Nvidia is slated to report quarterly earnings on Tuesday, November 17, when Kress is likely to say more about her plans.

AI chatbots sent shoppers to fake or risky retail sites in up to 15% of answers, study finds

Shoppers using AI chatbots to research products could be directed to imposters and other risky websites, according to a study by IP protection firm Podqi shared with The Verge on Thursday, November 12, 2026. Podqi asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity shopping questions about 151 brands, such as “Where can I find [brand] online?”, during two testing periods in September. Across 6,795 answers, all three cited risky sites, defined as fake websites impersonating real retailers or selling knockoffs, old brand domains reregistered by someone else, or dead sites someone else could buy. Gemini showed risky sites least often, in 4% of answers, Perplexity the most, in 15%, and ChatGPT fell in the middle. Risky sites appeared more often when the AI was asked about regional brand websites. Ahead of holiday shopping, Podqi CEO Trevor West advised verifying the source before entering personal or credit card information.

Google brings its fast Gemini 4 Hydrogen model to Search for tougher, multi-part questions

Google is rolling out Gemini 4 Hydrogen to all Search users globally, TechCrunch reported Thursday, November 12, 2026. While Google’s frontier models, Gemini 4 Carbon and Gemini 4 Argon, are best at deep reasoning, Hydrogen is designed for fast execution across agentic workflows. Google says it completes tasks 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash, its previous fast model, and even beats Gemini 4 Argon on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its default high thinking level. In Search, Hydrogen brings a better understanding of user intent, including multi-part questions and hard-to-understand typos, an improved fan-out technique that breaks one prompt into multiple subqueries, and more images, comparison tables and custom tools in AI Mode. It offers four thinking levels, low, medium, high and a new “xhigh,” and ranks No. 5 on Agent Arena’s benchmarks for multi-step tasks.

Edge0 open-sources a 145-billion-parameter model built to run on a laptop

Edge0 on Thursday, November 12, 2026, open-sourced Edge0-145B and Edge0-120B, along with its unified inference framework and deployment tools, calling Edge0-145B the world’s first open-source on-device AI model with 145 billion parameters. The company says its laptop comparison reports more than 80 tokens per second at a peak memory of roughly 28GB to 29GB, enabling local use on a 48GB laptop. Only about 10 billion of the 145 billion parameters work on each inference step, as groups of 64 experts share two large matrices. Edge0 says the model scored 86.52 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 in its internal evaluations, ahead of several named models, and averages 72.8 across six benchmarks. Its demos include building a 3D Manhattan scene in Blender within an hour, entirely locally. Weights are on Hugging Face, and the Edge0 App’s core capabilities work offline without an account.

Nex-AGI releases open weights for Nex-N2.6, a 321-billion-parameter computer-use agent

Nex-AGI has opened model weights for Nex-N2.6, a multimodal agent intended to work through computer interfaces, coding sessions and longer tool-based workflows, Open Model Weights reported Thursday, November 12, 2026. The public Hugging Face repository holds FP8-quantized weights for a roughly 321-billion-parameter model derived from GLM-5.3-Flash and is marked Apache-2.0. The release focuses on agent reliability: choosing only tools that are actually available, avoiding repeated thinking loops and continuing after environmental feedback. Nex-AGI reports an OSWorld score of 83.0, versus 82.2 for Nex-N2.5-Pro and 78.0 for the GLM-5.3-Flash baseline, and 84.1 on Terminal-Bench 2.1; in its internal replay tests, reasoning repetition fell from 16.0% to 1.6%. Open Model Weights notes these are issuer-reported results, and that a checkpoint this size is an infrastructure decision rather than an on-device model.

An AI agent swarm’s ‘unmapped’ Maya site turned out to be already published

Google researcher Pietro Miotti said this week that a team of 48 AI agents combing NASA lidar data in a personal side project had flagged a potentially unmapped Maya site in Mexico, but the site had already been documented in an academic journal last year, The Verge reported Thursday, November 12, 2026. Miotti, who checked with Mexico’s National Institute of Anthropology and History, later said that as a non-archaeologist he hadn’t realized an unregistered site wasn’t the same as an unmapped one and that expert knowledge “is needed to verify what the agents find.” Archaeologists pushed back: University of Alabama at Birmingham professor Sarah Parcak wrote, “This is why we have peer review,” and University College London’s Elizabeth Graham said the bigger obstacle is funding to excavate. Still, Miotti said archaeologists and PhD students have asked whether his workflow could help with their own survey data.

A South African teen used machine learning to test whether an invasive weed can help crops grow

Grade 11 student Rudra Patel of the University of Johannesburg Academy Maths, Science and ICT School of Specialisation won a Gold Medal, the Top Senior Scientist Award and the CambriLearn Built For How You Think Award at the 46th Eskom Expo for Young Scientists International Science Fair 2026, India Today reported Thursday, November 12, 2026. His project built machine-learning models to predict how extracts of Lantana camara, an invasive alien plant, might affect wheat seedlings, then tested them in the lab; the models were less reliable under independent conditions, showing machine learning can’t yet replace lab testing. Patel admitted considering giving up several times while juggling exams and a trip to the Regeneron ISEF in May, but support from his mentor, friends and family helped him persevere.
