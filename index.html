OpenAI cuts its funding round by $10 billion as the AI stock rout spooks investors

OpenAI is now seeking up to $20 billion in its latest round, down from a target of about $30 billion, CNBC confirmed on Saturday after the Financial Times first reported the cut. AI News

ChatGPT will fold Work into Chat in December, with one shared token budget

Starting in December, ChatGPT’s Plus, Pro, and Business plans will share a single token budget across ChatGPT and Codex as OpenAI moves Work’s agent features into the main app, according to an updated OpenAI support page reported by The Decoder. Bigger models and harder tasks use up the budget faster; when it runs out, users can wait for the reset, upgrade, or buy credits. Chats with Instant models won’t count against it, and you’ll be able to check your balance and reset time in Settings.

Models

SenseTime’s open Loop-9B runs the same layers twice to think deeper without getting bigger

SenseTime’s SenseNova team released SenseNova-Loop-9B-Preview, an open-weight model that reads text and images and is built for long documents, coding, and tool use. A block of 24 of its 32 layers runs twice in a row with the same weights, so it does 56 layers of work while storing only 32, and a technique called two-stream mHC manages how information flows through the loop. It was trained on sequences of up to 262,144 tokens and ships under the Apache 2.0 license on Hugging Face.

Models

Idol turns one picture into a 3D object in seconds, including the sides you can’t see

Sperid Labs released Idol, an open model that turns a single image of an object into a 3D Gaussian splat of up to about a million points, saved as a standard .ply file, and fills in the sides the picture never showed. It removes the background on its own, needs 7.2 GB of graphics memory at peak, and on a top data center GPU makes an object in about 3.4 seconds; on an RTX 4090 it takes about 19 seconds. Sperid’s own benchmark puts it ahead of TRELLIS and TripoSplat on most measures.

Innovation

DeepSeek open-sources a coding agent that takes requests and comes back with pull requests

DeepSeek’s Harness team open-sourced dsh-superagent, a research prototype that takes development requests through GitHub, and optionally the Feishu chat app, works on each in its own session and folder, and returns results such as pull requests to the original conversation, TechNode reported. It is set up around a single GitHub repository and includes a web panel showing what needs a human’s attention. The team says it grew out of its own daily workflow; it’s a prototype, not an official product, and it’s available under the MIT license.

Innovation

Google DeepMind’s PlanAssist cuts the time planners spend on home extension applications by up to 58%

PlanAssist, an AI planning tool built with Gemini by Google DeepMind with the UK government, Google Cloud, and Faculty, is expanding to 12 more English councils for a 12-month trial on live applications. In pilots at Barnet, Camden, and Dorset, it cut the average time officers spent assessing householder applications such as loft conversions and extensions by 58%, and officers approved more than 70% of its draft reports with no edits. It checks documents, drawings, heritage rules, and local policies and drafts cited reports, but planning officers keep the final say on every decision.

Motivation

Annoyed at learning about a school board vote secondhand, a student built an AI tool so no one else misses one

Ronit Singh, a student at Thomas Jefferson High School for Science and Technology in Virginia, only learned his school board had changed its cellphone rules when a friend told him. So he and two friends built Civic Cycle, a free website that spots newly published school board agendas, summarizes the meetings with Claude, and has a person review every summary “because we know that some people don’t trust AI fully,” WTOP reported. It covers Fairfax, Loudoun, Arlington, and Prince William counties and can send email alerts on topics you follow.
