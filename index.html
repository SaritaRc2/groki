Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO in a stunning reversal, and the stock closes up 31%

Nvidia on Monday announced Jensen Huang has been reinstated as CEO, effective immediately, CNBC reported on November 23, 2026, a dramatic twist in a scandal that had hammered the chipmaker’s stock. The board said it voted unanimously after “a series of discussions and meetings” with Huang, that it “received input from senior levels of the United States government,” and that it is confident his return “poses no national security risk.” Huang resigned earlier this month after reports of multiple extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy. “I am honored to return to NVIDIA and lead one of the most consequential technology companies in the world at the beginning of a new industrial revolution,” Huang said. President Donald Trump told CNBC Huang “did nothing wrong.” Shares closed up 31% after the news. The board’s independent investigation, run by Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz, “will proceed to conclusion.” Sen. Elizabeth Warren called the move “reckless,” and Senate Banking Chairman Tim Scott said Huang will publicly testify on Dec. 3.

U.S. adds Megaspeed and six other Southeast Asian companies to its trade blacklist

The U.S. has added Singapore-based Megaspeed and six other Southeast Asian companies to its trade blacklist, citing national security and foreign policy concerns, CNBC reported on November 24, 2026. In a Federal Register filing, the Commerce Department cited a “significant risk” of the companies being or becoming involved in diverting advanced computing products to unauthorized end users. License applications will be reviewed under a “presumption of denial.” The list also includes PT Megaspeed International, Malaysia’s MSFusion Sdn. Bhd. and Megaspeed (Thailand) Co., Ltd. Commerce opened its Megaspeed investigation in 2024; in May 2025 investigators requested records from Nvidia, which put Megaspeed on its “no sell” list the following month, and U.S. officials interviewed then-CEO Alice Huang during an on-site check in June 2025. Commerce also blacklisted an Alibaba subsidiary in Singapore, Apex Enterprise Solutions, Reuters reported; Alibaba said Apex never diverted restricted chips. “We do not want Singapore to be used and abused as a conduit by companies to circumvent America’s export controls,” Singapore Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said.

Amazon snaps up Decart’s AI optimization tech for a reported $7.5 billion as it readies Trainium sales

Amazon has agreed to acquire a good chunk of Israeli startup Decart, paying $7.5 billion according to Bloomberg, TechCrunch reported on November 23, 2026. Decart is known for interactive real-time world models, most recently Oasis 3, and AI video tools, but its standout product is the Decart Optimization Stack (DOS), software that helps AI models run efficiently on Nvidia, Amazon and Google hardware. Amazon said in a blog post that it will acquire the technology behind DOS along with Decart’s founding team and its AI researchers and engineers. “Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip,” said Peter DeSantis, Amazon’s SVP of foundational AI models, custom silicon and quantum computing. “More efficient compute means better training, better reinforcement learning, faster inference, and better models for customers at a lower cost.” Amazon, which has so far used its Trainium chips in its own data centers, is preparing to sell them to customers as an Nvidia competitor, and Decart’s technology could make it easier to move workloads onto Amazon chips.

Anthropic launches Claude Ship, letting AI agents review, fix and even approve code changes

Anthropic has launched Claude Ship, a set of tools that lets AI agents review, fix and approve code changes, TechCrunch reported on November 23, 2026. The offering combines Code Review, already generally available, with two new agents in beta: PR Steward, which tackles failing CI checks, merge conflicts and review feedback and comments after every push on what it changed and how to roll it back, and Claude Approvals, which applies an approval policy that a team writes in natural language as a file in its repository. “Most teams want a person to approve every change, and that’s easy to keep up with when people write every line. But a lot of developers build with agents now,” Anthropic’s blog post says. “On the Claude Code team, Claude writes most of our code, and we wanted everything after the PR to move that fast too.” Because it is in beta, Anthropic suggests running approvals in observe-only mode first, starting with one repository and setting monthly spending limits. Claude Ship is rolling out to Claude Team and Enterprise plans for GitHub repositories.

Signal tightens defenses against AI agents that read your screen, as banks and airlines also push back

Privacy-focused messaging app Signal is tightening protections against AI agents that can access information shown on users’ screens, CNBC reported on November 24, 2026. Signal said assistants built into mobile operating systems, such as Google’s Gemini and Apple’s Siri with onscreen awareness, can “scan” what is on screen and potentially store Signal data outside its control. It will block screenshots in its iOS app and, on Android, enable screen security when it detects an AI assistant trying to read the screen. “We want to be very clear: it is a serious problem that Signal — and application developers beyond — lack robust tools to prevent AI agents from undermining application-level privacy,” Signal Foundation president Meredith Whittaker wrote. Signal also warned third-party agents such as Meta’s Muse could reach private content through setup permissions. The move joins a broader push: United Airlines said Nov. 11 it would restrict unauthorized agents from booking tickets, and JPMorgan Chase, Morgan Stanley and Charles Schwab limit or block outside agents. AvePoint’s State of AI 2026 report found 88.4% of 750 surveyed organizations had at least one AI-agent-related security breach in the previous year.

Ant Group’s inclusionAI releases LingSante-3.1-flash, a 560B open-weight medical model

Ant Group’s inclusionAI team has published LingSante-3.1-flash, a healthcare-oriented open-weight language model for medical reasoning, research and longer tool-assisted tasks, Open Model Weights reported on November 23, 2026. Its Hugging Face model card names Ling-3.1-flash as the base and describes a hybrid-linear mixture-of-experts system with about 560 billion total parameters, around 25 billion active per token, and a claimed context window of up to one million tokens. The creators describe medical data woven through pretraining and post-training, reinforcement learning, evidence-oriented research workflows and multi-teacher distillation. The weights sit in a first-party Hugging Face repository under MIT licensing, with original FP8 mixed-precision files and a separate MXFP4 quantized variant. Serving it is a big job: the publisher’s SGLang instructions include setups with eight H200 GPUs or four GB300 GPUs. Benchmark results are the authors’ own, and the official documentation says the checkpoint is for research and evaluation, “is not a medical device,” and must not be used on its own for diagnosis, treatment, prescribing or triage.

SenseNova releases full open weights for RoboRSI-27B, a robot vision-language model

SenseNova has released the full weights of SenseNova-RoboRSI-27B, a vision-language model built for robotic manipulation and embodied agent workflows, Open Model Weights reported on November 23, 2026. The release succeeds an earlier Preview checkpoint and is available from the developer’s Hugging Face organization under Apache-2.0. Rather than having the language model emit every motor action, RoboRSI can call a separate vision-language-action policy, inspect a proposed movement trajectory, or write code that computes an end-effector target, while a RoboRSI Harness coordinates observation, planning and execution. According to the publisher’s model card, the full 27B model averages 34.7 on the RoboDojo simulator, compared with 10.0 for the Preview. SenseNova also reports experiments in dual-arm coordination. Open Model Weights notes these scores and robot trials are developer-reported, and that a real robot controller still needs hardware-specific validation, emergency-stop controls and human supervision. The release matters because it makes “a sophisticated embodied-agent checkpoint inspectable for outside evaluation.”

Hkimi experiment runs the 2.8-trillion-parameter Kimi K3.1 Pro on a single RTX 4090, at 0.17 tokens per second

An experimental inference project called Hkimi claims to run Moonshot AI’s roughly 2.8-trillion-parameter Kimi K3.1 Pro on a single consumer GPU without quantizing or pruning the checkpoint, Open Model Weights reported on November 23, 2026. Its author described the work in a Hugging Face community post. The setup used a rented RTX 4090 machine with a 64GB host-memory budget and about 2TB of NVMe storage, keeping most model data off the GPU and streaming the needed mixture-of-experts components during generation. The reported speed is about 0.17 tokens per second, roughly one token every six seconds, far below what interactive chat or coding agents need. Open Model Weights stresses that this is a community experiment with existing open weights, not a new Kimi release, and that the ‘without quantization’ claim needs reproduction. Still, it says the project shows how public weight access lets outside developers “explore unconventional serving architectures, even when those experiments are not commercially usable.”

One terminal command turns a junk-drawer Android tablet into a PC desk display

A single copied command in a Linux terminal was enough to pull a forgotten 8.4-inch Android tablet out of a kitchen junk drawer and turn it into a live PC desk display, Chip Dispatch reported on November 24, 2026. The tool is PhoneBox, an open-source project pairing a Linux desktop agent with an Android app; version 0.9.29 installed on an AllDoCube iPlay 50 mini Pro over USB. The install script downloads the software, verifies its checksum, offers to create a systemd user service, and can install the Android APK automatically, with developer mode on the tablet the only real prerequisite. The PhoneBox home screen shows PC system stats and what’s playing in the browser, and a second mode turns the tablet into a grid of tap-to-launch PC app tiles. “You weren’t hoarding old tech; you were waiting for the proper use,” wrote Ars Technica’s Nick Indge, who added that getting it running “couldn’t have been any simpler.” It turns a drawer cleanup into a hardware upgrade.

Fujitsu sees demand for its 2nm Monaka server chip outrunning its plans

Fujitsu expects to sell about 500,000 Monaka server processors over two years and projects ¥35 billion, roughly $220 million, in Monaka sales next fiscal year, Chip Dispatch reported on November 24, 2026. Chief technology officer Vivek Mahajan told Bloomberg that initial demand exceeds what the company anticipated, and that most of the 45 companies that received Monaka samples in August are expected to place orders. One customer running about 30,000 servers could need more than 200,000 processors, and Fujitsu is discussing production capacity with TSMC. Mahajan said customers in Japan, the U.S. and Europe want alternative technology for defense, finance, manufacturing and the public sector, citing data sovereignty and security. Monaka uses Arm’s architecture in a 3D-stacked design, with 2-nanometer compute cores above 5-nanometer cache and I/O, runs at up to 3.8GHz and supports 8,800MT/s memory; Fujitsu claims twice the AI inference throughput of other CPUs. The company targets ¥500 billion in annual chip revenue by 2035.

A school in Micronesia now takes coconuts instead of cash for tuition

Faith Christian Academy on Yap Island in Micronesia is accepting coconuts in lieu of tuition, easing the burden on families while helping rebuild the island’s once-flourishing coconut oil industry, Good News Network reported on November 24, 2026. “Yap is beautiful, but cash is hard to come by,” said founder Paul Zimmer. “We asked a simple question: what if the thing Yap has in abundance could help pay for the thing families want most for their kids?” Before, students sometimes resorted to dangerous nighttime fishing, and parents worked double shifts. “I felt relieved and happy about it because my dad didn’t have to work a lot for my tuition,” said student Shayalynn, whose father once worked double shifts to cover her schooling. Freddie Nole waits at the prison gates so no one walks out alone

Freddie Nole and his wife, Sue, meet men walking out of prison, often people with no one else coming for them, then take them to a meal, buy them clothes, get them a phone and drive them where they’re headed next, Good News Network reported on November 23, 2026. “Believe me, it never gets old,” Nole told GNN. What he treasures is “the appreciation that these individuals express to us for just being there, at a time when they’re most vulnerable.” His work is the subject of The Second Life of Freddie Nole, a JoyDrops documentary short by director Dana Nachman that rides along for a single day. Nole knows the road personally: he went to prison in 1969 and, on his own first day out, spent four hours at the parole office. “Those who were confined are the best teachers for those coming out,” he says, and one man he once picked up now picks up others himself. “We all want to think we can have redemption, that we can have second chances,” Nachman said. “That’s the crux of the story.”
