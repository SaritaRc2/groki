AI News

METR says Anthropic’s AI is improving about twice as fast, mostly with AI’s help—and Anthropic redacted most of the evidence

Independent evaluator METR reported on November 5, 2026 that, as of October 2026, Anthropic is likely improving model capabilities on well-specified tasks at roughly twice the March 2024–October 2025 rate, with high uncertainty (roughly 1.3× to 3×). AECI benchmark scores have grown at about 1.9× the pre-October 2025 rate [95% CI 1.5–2.8×]. METR guesses a majority—perhaps about 80%—of the current acceleration comes from Anthropic’s use of AI in R&D, and it saw no clear bottlenecks that would block further compounding over the next year. Anthropic redacted a majority of the evidence METR found most informative in the roughly 100-page assessment, citing competitor risk.

AI News

Claude’s Personal Finance beta lets U.S. Pro users connect banks via Plaid—read-only, no money moves

Anthropic began rolling out Personal Finance in Claude this week for Pro, Max, and Ultra plans in the U.S., letting users connect bank and card accounts through Plaid and ask about spending, bills, and balances in plain language. Access is read-only; the tool cannot move, manage, or invest money, and Anthropic says it has no ads or sponsored product pitches. Claude can see bank name, account details, balances, credit limits, and up to two years of transactions for linked accounts; chats inside the Personal Finance hub are excluded from model training. Anthropic’s primary article frames the feature as a first step toward working alongside licensed professionals rather than replacing them.

OpenAI’s BioBasicsBench scores GPT-6 Astra at 66.7% on everyday wet-lab data tasks

OpenAI introduced BioBasicsBench on November 6, 2026, a 100-task benchmark built with biotech firm Ligo that tests whether AI agents can interpret raw wet-lab evidence—gels, colony plates, microscopy, sequencing files, and instrument exports—rather than polished paper figures. At the xhigh reasoning setting, GPT-6 Astra earned 66.7% mean reward (95% CI 60.3–73.1%) versus 43.8% for GPT-5.6 Sol. Tasks span protein methods, imaging, quantification, nucleic acids, and molecular cloning, with rubric scoring for conclusions, measurements, and recognizing insufficient evidence.

Innovation

OpenAI’s GPT-6 Cyber finds 81 unknown Firefox flaws and stays in scope 99.93% of the time

OpenAI introduced GPT-6 Cyber on November 5, 2026, a cybersecurity model trained to find and validate exploitable vulnerabilities while staying inside researcher-set scope. On ExploitGym it scores 48.5%, up from 32.1% for GPT-5.6 Cyber, and in an alignment honeypot test it avoided out-of-scope exploits in 99.93% of cases without its deployment prompt and 100% with it. Researchers used it to develop 948 Linux privilege-escalation exploits and identify 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox vulnerabilities; Mozilla has begun shipping fixes. Classified Critical under OpenAI’s Preparedness Framework, it is initially available via API and Codex to approved Daybreak Red customers.

College of Charleston Honors student builds an offline AI app that narrates the world for blind users

College of Charleston Honors student Saif Chaudhry developed a real-time AI scene-narration mobile app through a Summer Undergraduate Research with Faculty grant, working with computer science professor Mia Wang. An iPhone 12 Mini streams video to a locally hosted model on a mid-tier PC; within seconds the phone speaks a natural description of nearby people, vehicles, buildings, and nature—running entirely offline to protect privacy and avoid cloud data centers. Chaudhry, a first-generation college student, has expanded the pipeline to a Duckietown robotic car and submitted a paper to the 2027 VISAPP conference; he graduates May 2027.
