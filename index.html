AI NewsLead

Google puts Argon on the table and keeps the public waiting

Alphabet's Google on Wednesday announced Argon, a larger model meant to anchor Gemini 4 and close ground on Anthropic and OpenAI. A spokesperson called it the company's most performant model yet for complex workloads, and said Google sees it as comparable to OpenAI's Astra and Anthropic's Opus on key coding and cyber benchmarks. Argon is going to select cybersecurity partners while Google joins a voluntary pre-release access process, with no public timeline given. Reuters also reported that Gemini 3.5 Pro, which Sundar Pichai had originally promised for June, is no longer planned, and that DeepMind founder Demis Hassabis has stepped aside as the lab was overhauled.

AI News

Dots clock in, with a human still on the send button

OpenAI used Tuesday's developer conference to debut Dots, always-on agents for Pro, Business Premium, and Enterprise subscribers, one assistant per person. A Dot runs on its own cloud computer, where it can browse, use connected apps, create files, and keep a project over time, but significant actions still come back to the user. Alexander Embiricos told Axios a Dot may draft a message and should not send it unless asked, and it will not finish some consequential financial transactions. Chief executive Sam Altman told reporters the aim is a middle path: not plowing ahead blindly, and not stopping, but keeping safety and monitoring ahead of capability.

AMD agrees to buy World Labs for about $8.2 billion in stock

AMD on Monday agreed to acquire World Labs, the San Francisco model lab led by Fei-Fei Li, in an all-stock deal valued at about $8.2 billion. The transaction is expected to close by the end of 2026, subject to regulatory approvals and other customary conditions. World Labs builds spatial-intelligence models that generate, reconstruct, and simulate interactive 3D environments, along with technology for robotic learning. After the close, Li is to become AMD's executive vice president and chief scientist, reporting to Lisa Su, while the team continues model research.

Models

Argon thinks in a million tokens, and only defenders see it first

Google's own account, published Wednesday by Koray Kavukcuoglu, treats Argon as a phased frontier model, not a public launch: trusted cyber defenders get it first through the Fairwind Program. The output limit rises to 1 million tokens from 64,000, at an introductory $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached input 95% off, before a later $4 and $20. Google reports 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1, first place at 51.3% on Zapier's AutomationBench, 91.7% on LVBench, and a tie for first at 68% on CWE-bench v1. Inside the company, Argon agents are credited with freeing more than 300 TiB of memory, with an estimated 500 TiB to 1 PiB still to come, and with a Rust libgav1 decoder running 2.7 times the prior Rust port.

Models

GPT-6.1 Sol prices itself at a fifth of Astra

OpenAI on Monday introduced GPT-6.1 Sol as an upgrade that nearly matches GPT-6 Astra on agentic coding, computer use, and professional work at one-fifth of Astra's standard input and output token prices. Cached input is listed at $0.10 per million tokens, 95% below standard input pricing and half of GPT-6 Sol's cached rate. On DeepSWE v1.1, OpenAI says Sol 6.1 matches Astra at roughly a fifth of the cost and beats GPT-6 Sol's best score by 6.4 points at lower effort. On a deliberately hard, non-representative factuality set, the share of answers containing an error at low reasoning effort fell from 11.4% to 7.7%.

Models

Sonnet 5.5 keeps the sticker price and spends fewer tokens

Anthropic on Monday released Claude Sonnet 5.5 as a faster complement to Opus 5.5 for scoped everyday tasks. The sticker price matches Sonnet 5, at $2 per million input tokens, $10 per million output tokens, and $0.20 per million for cache reads, but Anthropic says the new model usually needs fewer tokens, costs up to 30% less per task, and generates output more than 30% faster. On Terminal-Bench 4.0 it scores 70.6%, against Sonnet 5's 10.3%. Cyber capability comparable to Opus 5 means this is the first Sonnet to ship with that tier of cyber safeguards; Haiku 5.5 is still promised in the coming weeks.

Innovation

Quine spends a weekend ranking cancer-cell state shifts

Microsoft Research on Tuesday introduced Quine, an experimental system that joins a multimodal model of biology to tools, literature, and the scientists using them. Working with the Broad Institute, the team asked it to prioritize compounds predicted to shift pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma cells between states. In wet-lab assays of the classical-to-basal transition, the highest-ranked compounds produced the largest intended shifts, and the cut from a large search to a short list for the lab took one weekend. Microsoft says Quine is research technology, not a clinical product, and that access begins with a fellows program and selected collaborations.

Innovation

A phage enzyme system with a CRISPR-like repeat, function still unknown

On Sept. 23 Anthropic said Claude agents, given only a high-level prompt, flagged an undescribed system in bacteriophages: a reverse transcriptase already noted in earlier studies, a partner gene, and a long array of evenly spaced DNA repeats the lab is calling ART. About 950 agents spent 21 hours and 210 million tokens on the search before human scientists took the experiments. Anthropic says the function is still unknown, and that the Bay Area lab does not handle pathogens that infect people. Feng Zhang, a CRISPR pioneer at MIT and the Broad Institute, called the associated RNA-repeat arrays intriguing and worth further investigation.

Motivation

The side task comes back

A Sept. 16 note from OpenAI Economic Research reads 1.5 million work-related ChatGPT messages from April through July 2026 and asks whether people keep doing tasks outside their occupation. Among about 6,200 workers observed across those months, previously used cross-occupation tasks grew from 13.1% of occupation-specific AI activity in April to 25.9% in July. In matched one-month follow-ups, workers returned to such a task 23.6% of the time, against 8.4% for comparable workers with no observed use the month before. The implication is unsentimental: a job can widen, task by task, before anyone changes the title.

Motivation

CHROs call judgment essential. Most employees do not.

IBM's Institute for Business Value reported on Sept. 21, from a survey of 1,500 chief human resources officers and 8,800 employees, that executives and workers are not ranking the same skill. Seventy-one percent of CHROs called the ability to supervise, validate, and override AI outputs the workforce's most essential skill; only 29% of employees ranked judgment as important. Sixty percent of employees worry AI is eroding their skills, with critical thinking cited most often as the one in decline. Where workflows are explicitly human-led, AI-assisted, or AI-executed, those organizations reported 18% lower risk and 20% higher quality.
