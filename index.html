ChatGPT Wallet lets OpenAI’s AI agents shop and check out for you

OpenAI on Friday, November 13, 2026, launched ChatGPT Wallet, a feature that lets its AI agents, called dots, help users decide what to buy and then complete the purchase on their behalf, TechCrunch reported. It starts with a limited number of U.S. ChatGPT Pro subscribers, ages 18 and up, with a U.S.-issued Visa, Mastercard or American Express card. Users review the items, the total and the payment card before checkout and approve each purchase with a security check such as Face ID or a fingerprint. The wallet then charges the card with a payment token, a substitute for the real card details, so they aren’t shared with the dot or the merchant, and shoppers keep card rewards and dispute protections. OpenAI plans to let users set spending limits and pre-approve some purchases later. One hurdle: some retailers block AI agents, and the largest, Amazon, steers shoppers to its own Alexa agent. OpenAI also published a Commerce Partner Playbook for retailers.

Researcher finds export-banned Nvidia Blackwell ‘fingerprints’ in China’s top open AI models

Leading open-weight AI models from Chinese companies appear to have been trained on Nvidia Blackwell chips that the U.S. bars from shipping to China, according to new research reported by TechCrunch on Friday, November 13, 2026. Will DePue, a former OpenAI researcher who leads the nonprofit research group Unemployment Labs, analyzed the weights of open models from DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group, and found compelling evidence that closely watched releases, including Moonshot’s Kimi K3 models and DeepSeek’s V4 models, bear fingerprints of training on Blackwell chips. TechCrunch notes that doesn’t mean the models were trained entirely on Nvidia chips or that export laws were necessarily broken, since the chips could sit in offshore data centers, such as in Malaysia. Epoch AI estimates about two million advanced chips entered China in 2024 and 2025 through schemes designed to hide their final destination. The findings add pressure on Nvidia, whose CEO Jensen Huang left this week after admitting an affair with Alice Huang, an executive of Megaspeed, a company implicated in smuggling Nvidia chips into China.

FAA lifts a four-hour nationwide ground stop as officials look at whether its new AI airspace system played a role

The FAA halted takeoffs across the U.S. on Friday, November 13, 2026, issuing a national ground stop at 3:44 p.m. ET “due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety,” then lifting it at 8:15 p.m. ET, more than four hours later, The Verge reported. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed an aging air traffic control system, saying analog telecom switches and routers “got overloaded with data,” and he and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin confirmed that “patches” to the existing system sparked the issue. Asked about a possible link to SMART (Strategic Management of Airspace, Routes and Trajectories), a cloud-based AI system activated nationwide on November 10, Mullin said officials were looking into it. Air Space Intelligence, which designed SMART under a 12-year, $875 million contract, denied any connection. Mullin said there was no reason to suspect a cyberattack. FlightAware listed 9,124 U.S. flights delayed and 5,901 canceled as of 8:39 p.m. ET.

Amazon reportedly plans a ‘World Product Graph’ indexing products sold anywhere online, for people and AI agents

Amazon has plans to build a vault of data on every product sold anywhere on the internet, even items it doesn’t sell, and make it available through its own site, The Verge reported Friday, November 13, 2026, citing Reuters. The project, called the “World Product Graph,” is meant to keep shoppers from leaving Amazon to research products on Reddit or Google, and to make AI shopping agents more likely to rely on Amazon’s index. It would also feed Amazon’s AI shopping assistant, Alexa for Shopping, previously called Rufus: an internal document says “Many Rufus answers are factually wrong or incomplete,” and running searches through the AI is expensive, so more data is meant to make it more accurate and able to use cheaper models. The plan reportedly depends on discovering how brands organize products on their own websites, and the document lists an October 1, 2027 release date, though Reuters says it’s unclear if or when it will ship. Amazon told Reuters draft documents “often paint an incomplete picture.”

Thinking Machines releases open weights for Inkling 2 Small, a 594-billion-parameter multimodal model

Thinking Machines Lab has made the weights of Inkling 2 Small downloadable, completing the move from the model’s November 5 hosted-only introduction, Open Model Weights reported Friday, November 13, 2026. A November 12 update to the company’s launch article points to a new Hugging Face repository where the checkpoint is labeled Apache 2.0. Inkling 2 Small is a sparse mixture-of-experts multimodal model with 594 billion total parameters and about 17 billion active per token; its 48-layer decoder routes each token to eight of 384 experts and accepts text and images, answering in text. The model card describes up to one million tokens of context, while the launch article discussed a 256K served context. It is no laptop model: the official minimum is 500GB of combined GPU memory for the native NVFP4 checkpoint or 800GB for MXFP8, such as two B300 or four H200 GPUs. Open Model Weights notes the benchmark tables are the publisher’s own and haven’t been independently replicated.

G-STAR open models generate a 1024x1024 image in 2.5 seconds on a single GPU, researchers say

A team from Peking University’s DAGroup, together with Meituan Longcat, SJTU and co-authors, has released G-STAR, an open framework for autoregressive image generation that produces a 1024x1024 image in 2.5 seconds on a single Nvidia H200, mlllm.io reported Saturday, November 14, 2026. The researchers say that is about 20 times faster than earlier discrete autoregressive models: 21.57 times faster than Janus-Pro-7B and 8.36 times faster than Emu3.5-34B. Weights for two models and inference code are on Hugging Face: G-STAR-Pro-8B, with 32x compression, and G-STAR-Flash-1.7B, with an effective 64x compression. Pro-8B can also edit images from text instructions, scoring 7.45 on GEdit-Bench versus 7.53 for GPT-Image-1. The approach packs more codes into each compressed position, so far fewer steps are needed per picture. mlllm.io cautions that all quality scores are author-reported and that the speed comparison is against older autoregressive models, not modern diffusion systems.

Pruna AI’s free adapters erase objects from photos, shadows and reflections included

Pruna AI has released P-Q-Image-2.1-Remove-Object, a set of open LoRA adapters for the Qwen-Image-2.1 image model that remove an object from a photo along with its shadow and reflection without shifting the remaining pixels, mlllm.io reported Friday, November 13, 2026. Shadows and reflections are removed even outside the marked area. Three variants are free on Hugging Face: distilled versions that finish in 6 or 8 steps instead of 40 and run without classifier-free guidance, plus a 40-step base version. The fast variants stack the task adapter on top of Pruna’s distillation LoRA. Ready-made ComfyUI workflows, a custom node and an online demo in Pruna’s playground are also available. Pruna lists limitations up front: with a rough mask, the model may remove a neighboring object, and the filled area can look smoother than its surroundings, with a faint mesh-like artifact. The release includes no quantitative measurements of quality, speed or memory needs.

Drogue raises $20 million to power floating AI data centers with the ocean’s heat

Drogue, a six-month-old startup in Washington, D.C., is building a system that harvests heat from the ocean to power data centers aboard floating vessels, TechCrunch reported exclusively on Friday, November 13, 2026. The company said it has raised $20 million from investors including XYZ Venture Capital, Toyota Ventures and Slow Ventures. Its approach, ocean thermal energy conversion, draws in warm surface water to cool the servers, uses that warmed water to boil ammonia that drives a turbine, and condenses the ammonia with cold water pumped from 1,000 meters deep. Founder and CEO Sam Sipe, a former Navy E-2D Hawkeye pilot, says data centers run on 8- to 10-year real-estate timelines while “AI demand has increased 10x in just the last three years.” Drogue has designed a multi-megawatt prototype to be installed on a traditional oceangoing ship and sent to sea in early 2028. It has preliminary approval from the American Bureau of Shipping and is buying its first ship.

Cloudflare open-sources Kitesurf, a Chromium-free browser engine for AI agents

Cloudflare has open-sourced Kitesurf, an experimental browser engine for AI agents that runs on Cloudflare Workers without Chromium or browser containers, mlllm.io reported Friday, November 13, 2026. The code is on GitHub under the Apache 2.0 license. Instead of farms of headless Chromium containers, Kitesurf assembles a rendering stack from Rust and WebAssembly components: Blitz for the DOM and layout, Stylo for CSS, Boa for JavaScript, Linebender for text and 2D graphics and Krilla for PDFs. With it, the engine parses HTML, applies CSS, runs JavaScript and renders screenshots, PDFs and HTML. It talks to outside tools through the standard Chrome DevTools Protocol, so Puppeteer or an AI agent can connect to it. It can be run locally for free, though deploying it to a Cloudflare account requires a paid Workers plan. mlllm.io notes that claims of lower automation costs haven’t been measured yet, and it is unknown how many real websites render correctly.

Singapore teens win Samsung’s Solve for Tomorrow with a smartwatch app that helps students with special needs through moments of distress

Four students from Temasek Secondary School, Saanvi Gupta, Ayesha Nadya, Malyanah Rizwana and Sakeenah Banu, won the Grand Prize in Samsung Solve for Tomorrow 2026 in Singapore for WristAssured, a smartwatch app that helps students with special educational needs recognize and manage moments of distress, Samsung Newsroom Singapore announced Tuesday, November 10, 2026. Using a Galaxy Watch’s sensors, the app reads heart rate, heart-rate variability, sweat activity, skin temperature and movement against each student’s own baseline. When it detects stress, it checks in with gentle vibrations and calming visual prompts, and if distress persists it alerts SEN officers or teachers. “Technology should adapt to the individual rather than have the individual adapt to the technology,” Gupta said. The team won S$7,000 and S$10,000 in Samsung products. Third place went to Pei Hwa Secondary School’s SafeSignal, a wearable that helps nonspeaking autistic children signal for help in emergencies. The contest drew 116 entries.
