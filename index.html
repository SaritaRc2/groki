Homeland Security orders DeepSeek out of every federal civilian agency, including third-party tools built on its models

The Department of Homeland Security made public an order, published in the Federal Register on Tuesday, October 20, that bars products and services from China’s DeepSeek from federal civilian agency purchases and orders all DeepSeek and DeepSeek-affiliate products removed from civilian agency IT systems. The order reaches past DeepSeek’s own apps: third-party products and services developed using DeepSeek implementations must come out too. The signed Exclusion and Removal Order #2026-01, issued under the Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act and first released to agencies on July 24, says DeepSeek’s servers sit in China, that using its prompt-and-response apps could send sensitive data on U.S. government employees or vendors there, and that the models themselves could contain malware or security vulnerabilities. National security and secure compartmented systems are left to the Defense Department and the intelligence community. The notice landed days after DeepSeek shipped V4.1-Flash-1017.

Anthropic commits 5% of its shares to a new Public Benefit Fund, with 2% more for every 50% jump in its value

Anthropic said on Sunday, October 18, that it will put 5% of its shares into the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, subject to stockholder approval, and that the board may add another 2% for every 50% increase in the company’s value above a set baseline. The fund is a wholly owned LLC rather than a charity, so it can switch between kinds of giving, from large donations to nonprofits to direct payments to specific groups; Anthropic named the labor-market effects of AI as one example, on top of the $200 million it has already committed to that problem. None of the fund’s Anthropic equity will be used for lobbying or political giving. Counting its co-founders’ pledge to donate 80% of their equity and an employee donation match that about half of staff use, Anthropic says roughly 20% of its shares are now dedicated to the public good. Governance details and the valuation milestones are due in the coming months.

MiniMax M3.1 Flash: near-DeepSeek smarts at $0.15 per million tokens, but it rarely admits when it doesn’t know

Chinese lab MiniMax released M3.1 Flash on October 19, an agent model for business workflows, coding, and computer use with a one-million-token context window, priced at $0.15 per million input tokens and $0.60 per million output, VentureBeat reported. Artificial Analysis scored it 42 on its Intelligence Index, up from 29 for MiniMax M3, while cutting the average cost per task from about $0.51 to $0.22; DeepSeek V4.1-Flash-1017 scores 45. The catch is reliability: on the AA-Omniscience test it answered 56% of questions correctly, but when it was wrong it almost never admitted uncertainty, a 93% hallucination rate under that benchmark’s definition. MiniMax says the weights, proprietary at launch, will be released October 23, with license terms not yet given, and that an earlier version ran anonymously as “Space Bunny” and processed more than 200 trillion tokens.

Meta’s Muse Glimmer 30B is an open, Apache-licensed agent model built to run on one consumer GPU

Meta published Muse Glimmer on October 19, an open-source 30-billion-parameter model with a perception encoder, built for autonomous agent tasks on consumer hardware with no cloud required. It takes text and image input, was trained on data in more than a hundred languages, and is quantized to about 4-bit precision so it can run on a single GPU. It folds multi-step reasoning, tool use, and recovering from failures into one model, and Meta says it beats similar-sized models such as Gemma4-31B and Qwen3.6-27B on tool use, multi-step planning, and agent task completion. It is released under the Apache 2.0 license, which allows commercial use.

Inception’s Mercury 3 writes by diffusion instead of word by word, claiming 1,068 tokens a second

Inception launched Mercury 3 on October 19, which it calls the most capable diffusion-based language model yet: instead of producing text one token at a time, it refines a whole answer in parallel. The company reports 1,068 tokens per second on Nvidia GPUs, four to eight times faster than speed-focused frontier models, and says it matches or beats GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5 on a range of benchmarks. It is more than 40 times larger than Mercury 1, has a one-million-token context window, and costs $0.20 per million input tokens and $0.70 per million output, half that during launch. Early users include a tutoring app that says hints and marked answers now come back in about a second. It is available through Inception’s API, OpenRouter, Baseten, and Vercel.

Innovation

Four Gemini 4 Argon agents beat Minecraft’s Ender Dragon in 27 hours, writing every program themselves

Google researchers put four Gemini 4 Argon coding agents in a randomly generated Minecraft survival world on Easy difficulty with one goal: defeat the Ender Dragon. Nobody assigned roles or wrote game logic; each agent controlled its player by writing JavaScript programs through the open-source Mineflayer library and talked to teammates in the game chat. Over 27 hours and 14 minutes, the team wrote 2,783 programs, exchanged 229 messages, and died 605 times, then won after one agent worked out that the dragon kept healing from crystals on top of the pillars and found a way to climb up and destroy them without a pickaxe. A fifth agent watched as an observer and flagged no cheating. The team estimates the run cost about $520 at Gemini 4 Argon’s introductory API prices, and notes its limits: the library tells agents exactly what blocks and creatures surround them, and this was a single run.

Innovation

Amazon’s TorchNeuron lets PyTorch code run on its Trainium chips with one setting change, open-sourced today

Amazon announced TorchNeuron on October 19, a native PyTorch backend that registers its Trainium AI chips as a standard PyTorch device, so existing code moves over by changing one configuration setting instead of being rewritten. Amazon says 85% of AI researchers and developers write in PyTorch, and that the real cost of switching chips is usually the engineering time to port and revalidate code. TorchNeuron supports eager mode for research and debugging, torch.compile for production speed, and custom kernels on Trainium2 and Trainium3, and it is released under the Apache 2.0 license at the PyTorch Conference North America on October 20, starting as a public beta. Early users span voice AI, robotics, video, self-driving, and drug discovery.

Motivation

Died 605 times, still won: turn every loss into a rule, the way the Minecraft agents did

The four Gemini agents that beat Minecraft did not win by avoiding failure. They died 605 times, and 22% of their programs ended in errors. What they did after each setback is the lesson: when a fireball destroyed a pile of dropped items, one agent posted a rule for the team, and two minutes later another posted the root cause and a fix. After 69 deaths to the dragon, they wrote down rules between attempts, and a warning about one tool quirk turned into a helper function used in 655 programs. When one agent’s program claimed a success that hadn’t happened, it corrected itself two minutes later. Try it this week: after your next mistake, write one sentence about what you’ll do differently, keep those sentences in one place, and reread them before you start again.

Motivation

“I don’t know” is a strong answer: why a model that guesses on everything scores worse than it should

MiniMax’s new M3.1 Flash got 56% of Artificial Analysis’s factual questions right, more than DeepSeek’s or Z.ai’s cheap models. But it still ended with an overall knowledge score of just 16, because when it didn’t know, it nearly always guessed instead of saying so, VentureBeat reported. The test penalizes confident wrong answers, while an honest “I’m not sure” costs less. The same holds for people learning something new: saying where your knowledge ends is what lets a teacher, a teammate, or a search fill the gap, while a confident guess hides the gap. Today, when someone asks you something you’re unsure about, say what you do know, say what you don’t, and go find out.
