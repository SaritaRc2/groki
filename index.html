AI NewsLead

The King vs. Claude: Elvis Presley’s music owners sue Anthropic over 274 songs

The owners of Elvis Presley’s music catalog sued Anthropic on Friday in federal court in Northern California, accusing it of copying 274 of his songs without permission to train its Claude models. The plaintiffs, led by a subsidiary of Authentic Brands Group plus music publishers and songwriters’ trusts, name hits like “Can’t Help Falling in Love,” “All Shook Up,” “Don’t Be Cruel,” and “Return to Sender.” They say Anthropic kept the lyrics in an internal library for training future models, and that Claude still reproduces them: asked to recreate songs “for a modern audience,” it allegedly returned the “same structure, same hooks reused with light rewording.” They seek up to $41.1 million in statutory damages, $150,000 per song, plus more for stripping copyright information, along with an injunction. The Guardian calls it one of the first major challenges brought directly on behalf of an artist’s catalog rather than by labels. Anthropic told NBC News it disagrees with the claims and intends to defend the case fully.

AI News

Pennsylvania now makes ads say so when AI made them

Gov. Josh Shapiro signed the Artificial Intelligence Deceptive Advertising Act, Senate Bill 806, on October 22, among 58 bills he signed that day. The law bars advertisers from using synthetic, AI-generated content in a way that could create a false impression about the goods or services on offer, and any synthetic content used must carry a “clear and conspicuous disclosure” saying the ad was generated or modified with AI. Its sponsor, state Sen. Nick Pisciottano, called it “a major step for consumer protection in Pennsylvania” that lets shoppers make informed decisions “while still allowing businesses to take advantage of this technology.”

SpaceX says it closed $40 billion in debt to buy AI chips, and warns neoclouds face a shakeout

Elon Musk said Sunday on X that SpaceX has closed $40 billion in debt financing to buy graphics processors for AI, crediting its investment-grade rating and its Starlink and launch revenue. MiniMax M3.1 Flash: a fast, cheap agent model that once ran in secret as “Space Bunny”

MiniMax rolled out M3.1 Flash, and revealed that “Space Bunny,” a mystery model that processed more than 200 trillion tokens while running anonymously, was an early version of it. MiniMax says it runs nearly three times faster and cuts agent-task costs by 70% versus its predecessor, at $0.15 per million input tokens and $0.60 per million output tokens. The company reports 73.7% on the DeepSWE coding test, 61.7 on OSWorld V2 for operating a computer, and 82% on the CyberGym security benchmark, with typical output speeds above 170 tokens a second. These are MiniMax’s own numbers. It’s available through MiniMax’s API and apps now, with weights promised later and a bigger Pro model still to come.

Models

Small specialists: Mistral’s Timestral predicts what comes next, and Tencent’s Hy-Memory decides what agents remember

Two of last week’s open releases aren’t chatbots at all. Mistral’s Timestral-209M is a forecasting model for time series, like sales or sensor readings: it comes in four sizes from 5 million to 209 million parameters, reads up to 32,768 past steps, and predicts up to 1,024 steps ahead with ranges instead of a single guess. Tencent Hunyuan’s Hy-Memory-30B-A3B, with 30 billion parameters but only about 3 billion active at a time, works inside agent memory systems such as Mem0 and MemOS, deciding what to keep and how new information updates what an agent already knows. Both are on Hugging Face under open licenses.

Innovation

A robot that rewrites its own playbook beat a frontier model at hands-on tasks, at about 1% of the cost

A University of Hong Kong MMLab team built PhysicalRSI 1.0, short for Physical Recursive Self-Improvement, and it ranked first on the RoboDojo robot benchmark with a 31.38% average task success rate, versus 22.21% for GPT-6 Astra, at roughly 1% of Astra’s token cost, the team says. The trick: the AI model’s weights stay frozen, and instead a “System 2” model studies failures and rewrites the robot’s working program, its tools, memory, and skills, while a “System 1” layer does the physical moves. Better versions are kept for the next generation. It hit 92.67% on a block-swapping task where Astra scored 18%, and in cloth folding its success climbed from 27.5% to 60% across generations.

Grok’s AI-made “Odyssey” sets sail with a seven-minute first episode, and critics aren’t on board yet

Three months after Elon Musk promised a full-length, “historically accurate” AI version of “The Odyssey,” Grok Imagine released a seven-minute “Episode 1” made with Wonder Studios and director Dev Walker. TechCrunch’s Anthony Ha found some shots convincing enough to pass for real actors on a real sea, but said the clip lacks “any sense of artistry, inspiration, or human interest,” with long narration and stilted dialogue. The contrast is with Christopher Nolan’s practical-effects “Odyssey,” a box office hit since July. It’s a real test of how far AI video has come, and how far it still has to go to tell a story people care about.

Motivation

“You don’t need to be Spider-Man to deserve a seat at the table”

Palantir CEO Alex Karp, who is dyslexic, says neurodivergent people have a future in the AI era. Daniel Wendler, a psychologist who is himself autistic, told Fortune Karp has a point, just not the “genius savant” one. Workplaces were built around neurotypical strengths, he says, and AI is a chance to redesign them, with tools that help break big assignments into smaller steps, organize tasks, and support communication. “If you don’t have neurodivergent minds in the room tackling AI, then you miss out on that unique perspective,” he said. And his line worth keeping: “A person does not need to be a genius superhuman, you know, Spider-Man, to be able to have something to offer and to deserve a seat at the table.”

Motivation

AI is a great calculator, but “intelligence is mostly judgment”

Apollo chief economist Torsten Slok looked at the S&P 500 minus its seven tech giants and found flat profit margins and revenue per worker, saying AI has delivered “zero financial payoff” to corporate America outside tech so far. His reason is the useful part: “AI may be a great calculator, but in business, intelligence is mostly judgment.” Accountability, trust, culture, unwritten rules, and fairness still come from people. The tools keep getting cheaper; knowing what to do with them is still your job.
