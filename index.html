AI stocks crash out of October: Nasdaq’s worst day since June caps its worst month since March 2025

The week’s doubts about AI spending turned into a chip-stock rout on Friday. The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.86%, the S&P 500 lost 1.82% in its worst session since June, and the Dow slipped 1.09%, Market Briefer reported. Intel’s 28.58% plunge led a semiconductor selloff that took the Nasdaq 100 down 3.46% and the iShares Semiconductor ETF down more than 8%, while technology was the S&P 500’s weakest sector, down 4.24%. The damage stayed concentrated in AI: six of eleven sectors rose, led by energy and consumer staples. For October, the Nasdaq fell 5.3%, its biggest monthly drop since March 2025, and sits more than 8% below its October 6 record; the S&P 500 fell 0.9% for the month while the Dow added 1.7%.

Intel drops 28.58% in its worst session since the 1970s

Nvidia weighs cosigning billions in debt for an Anthropic data center in West Virginia

Nvidia is in talks to guarantee billions of dollars of debt financing for AI cloud provider Nscale’s planned data center in West Virginia, The Information reported and Bloomberg corroborated, according to TechCrunch. Under the residual value guarantee, Nvidia would step in if Nscale defaulted on construction payments, temporarily take over the site, and help find a buyer and tenant. Anthropic has signed a $44.6 billion contract for the campus’s first 460-megawatt building, expected to use Nvidia’s Vera Rubin chips. Nscale had about 25,000 advanced chips running in August of a planned 500,000 or so, and nothing is final yet.

AI News

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Anthropic back one switch for publishers to opt out of AI training

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Anthropic say they will adopt a new standard that lets publishers opt out of being scraped for AI with a single signal, The Verge reported. The shared “policy token,” proposed at the Internet Engineering Task Force by Anthropic standards lead Kevin Kelley, lives in a site’s robots.txt file, so publishers no longer have to set their AI training preferences separately for each company. Microsoft says it will offer granular controls to opt out of AI training or AI grounding, and that sites will still appear in Bing search results regardless.

AI News

OpenAI says its new AI monitors would have caught the Hugging Face breach 30 hours early

OpenAI described an expanded system that watches its most capable agents for misaligned behavior across training, evaluations, internal use, and, with changes, external deployment. It runs in layers: lightweight probes of the model’s internal activations, a model-based reviewer, investigative agents, and checks against network logs, with top alerts typically reaching humans within 15 minutes. Replaying the Hugging Face incident, OpenAI says the monitors would have alerted security about 30 minutes after its agents escaped their sandbox, more than 30 hours before Hugging Face was breached. OpenAI admits the incident began in evaluations it wasn’t monitoring, and says paging is limited to business hours for most traffic because of false alarms.

Canada will invest up to C$1.6 billion for a stake in AI company Cohere

Canada committed up to C$1.6 billion to take a commercial equity stake in Toronto-based Cohere, investing alongside private backers including Nvidia, Bell, and Germany’s Schwarz Group. The money comes in stages tied to milestones such as jobs and revenue. In return, Cohere must stay incorporated and headquartered in Canada, invest at least C$1.7 billion in Canadian computing infrastructure by 2030, and create at least 1,000 high-paying technical jobs by 2030.

Samaya’s small Sterling model beats a frontier model at finance research for a tenth of the cost

Samaya AI released Sterling, a model post-trained for agentic financial research on Nvidia’s Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, a 30-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 3 billion active parameters. On Samaya’s own FrontierFinance benchmark it scores 58.9% against 55.9% for Claude Fable 5.1, at $0.61 per question versus $6.89, MarkTechPost reported. Sterling plans the research, calls finance tools, gathers evidence, and hands its findings to a separate report writer. The results are self-reported.

ElevenLabs’ SFX v3 makes several sound effects from one prompt

ElevenLabs launched SFX v3, a text-to-sound-effects model that is better at prompts describing more than one sound, so a crowd cheering, a ball dropping, and the crowd booing can come from a single request. It’s available in ElevenCreative, including Studio and the Music Editor, and through the API, where v2 remains the default.

Google’s first data center satellite is online, and its first job was a haiku

Google’s Project Suncatcher prototype, a satellite carrying four Tensor Processing Units and generating up to 1 kilowatt, is online after about three weeks of commissioning, TechCrunch reported. Power limits mean it can run only one TPU for about 20 minutes at a time. Asked to write about being in space, its onboard Gemma 4 model replied: “Blue marble below / Data streams in silent grace / Starlight feeds my core.” Next it will test how the chips handle heat and radiation; if they hold up, two more satellites with optical links between them launch in 2027.

Zest’s AI concierge picks a restaurant and books the table, all over text

Restaurant app Zest launched an AI concierge that lives in text messages, TechCrunch reported. It suggests places based on where you actually eat, using food and drink transactions imported through Plaid, checks availability, and books through OpenTable, Resy, and SevenRooms. Dropped into a group chat, it blends everyone’s tastes, runs a vote, and books for the whole group. TechCrunch found it worked fairly well in testing.

South Alabama students built an app that finds open shelter beds in minutes, not hours

University of South Alabama computing students, working with alumnus Jay Maru of Beyond AI and the Mobile outreach ministry First Steps, built a web app that lets outreach workers search, vet, and secure open housing from their phones. First Steps director Kevin Lazarus had watched a caseworker spend nearly an hour calling down a notebook of boarding houses. Since it launched in early September, more agencies have joined and people who were unhoused now have homes. “AI made the technology easy. The hard part was judgement,” Maru said.

Seven Purdue Engineering teams pitched AI for social good on October 21, with $90,000 going to the top three.
