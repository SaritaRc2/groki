AI News

Google will switch off AI for K-12 students by default, but schools that keep AI off lose Search too

Google said Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search will be entirely off for K-12 student accounts until school administrators turn them on, Engadget reported. Administrators can enable the products one at a time, but Google’s education chief Chris Phillips told The New York Times that “Search and AI are deeply integrated,” so students at schools that ban AI tools will not have access to Search. The change walks back an August move that turned on some AI features in Classroom by default and drew educator backlash. Google is also bringing Guided Learning, which walks students through homework step by step instead of giving the answer, to Search in student accounts, starting with select U.S. schools this month.

Models

Soniox STT v6 listens to both sides of the conversation, so voice agents stop interrupting themselves

Soniox released STT v6, a speech recognition model that can take in the text a voice agent is speaking and use it as context for understanding the user’s reply. The same feature lets v6 filter the agent’s own voice out of the microphone feed while still hearing users who interrupt, which Soniox calls agent voice echo cancellation. The company says v6 is more accurate across more than 60 languages for voice typing, meeting transcription, speaker identification, and real-time translation. It comes in both real-time and asynchronous versions.

Innovation

Anthropic’s FlashProve can mathematically prove which AI model wrote an answer, without revealing its secrets

Anthropic released FlashProve, an open-source research prototype for “provable inference”: a cryptographic proof that a response came from a specific committed model, while revealing nothing about the model’s weights. Anthropic says Claude helped produce the improvements, and the team backed the key security claims with proofs in the Lean formal verification language. Tested on the 400-billion-parameter Llama 4 Maverick, a single H100 GPU generates proofs at about 110 tokens per second, and a regular CPU can check each roughly 19 MB proof in about 17 seconds. Anthropic says the work could help catch secretly swapped models or tampered servers, though it is limited for now to 1,024-token contexts.
